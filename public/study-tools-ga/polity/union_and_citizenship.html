Article 1: Name and Territory of the Union & Type of Polity

Topics at a Glance
Article 1 Name & Territory of the Union
Clause (1) • Two Official Names of Our Country

"India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States."

💡 Hinglish: Samvidhan mein desh ke sirf 2 official naam hain — India aur Bharat. Aur hamara desh Federation nahi balki "Union of States" (राज्यों का संघ) hai.

Clause (2): The States and the territories thereof shall be as specified in the First Schedule.
Clause (3): The "Territory of India" (भारत का राज्यक्षेत्र) shall comprise of 3 things:
(a) States Territories of the States (28)
(b) Union Territories UTs specified in First Schedule (8)
(c) Acquired Such other territories as may be acquired
⭐ Super Important Concept

India vs USA: Destructible or Indestructible?

🇮🇳 INDIA: "An Indestructible Union of Destructible States" 👉 Union (Desh) kabhi toot nahi sakta, lekin Parliament kisi bhi State ka naam, area ya boundary badal/tod sakti hai!
🇺🇸 USA (America): "An Indestructible Union of Indestructible States" 👉 USA mein bina us State ki manzoori ke uski boundary nahi badli ja sakti.
💡 "Territory of India" vs "Union of India": Union of India mein sirf States (28) aate hain, jabki Territory of India ek bada term hai jisme States + UTs + Future Acquired Territories teeno aate hain!

Type of Polity: Why India is called a "Union of States" and NOT a "Federation of States"?

1. Federation of States (Ex: USA)

By Treaty/Agreement

• A new state/country that is formed through a treaty or an agreement between various independent federating units (states).

(Jaise America mein 13 azaad colonies ne aapas mein samjhauta/agreement karke USA banaya).

2. Union of States (Ex: INDIA)

No Right to Secede

• No agreement between the federating units; that means States do NOT have the right to secede (alag hone ka adhikar nahi) from the Union.

(Dr. B.R. Ambedkar ne bataya ki Indian Federation kisi agreement ka natija nahi hai, isliye koi bhi rajya Bharat se alag nahi ho sakta!)

Pages 2 & 3 • Powers of Parliament Over States

Articles 2, 3 & 4, Berubari Case (1960) & 100th CAA (2015)

Topics at a Glance
Article 2 External / Foreign Territory

Admission or Establishment of New States

"Parliament may by law admit into the Union, or establish, new states on such terms and conditions as it thinks fit."

💡 Hinglish Explanation: Jab Bharat kisi bahari kshetra (Foreign Territory) ko Bharat mein shamil karta hai jo pehle India ka hissa nahi tha (Jaise Sikkim ko 1975 mein shamil kiya gaya), toh wo Article 2 ke tahat hota hai.

🔍 Difference Between Article 2 and Article 3:
• Article 2: Incorporating foreign territory into India.
• Article 3: Internal changes in the existing States of India.
Article 3 Internal Re-adjustment of Existing States

Formation of New States and Alteration of Areas, Boundaries, or Names of Existing States

Parliament may by law do 5 things (🧠 Trick: "F - I - D - A - A"):

a Form a new State by separation of territory from any State OR by uniting two or more States/parts of States OR by uniting any territory to a part of any State (Jaise Bihar se Jharkhand alag karna).
b Increase the area of any State (Kisi rajya ka kshetrafal badhana)
c Diminish the area of any State (Kisi rajya ka kshetrafal ghatana)
d Alter the boundaries of any State (Seema badalna)
e Alter the name of any State (Jaise Orissa se Odisha)
⚠️ Important Note on Article 3 Bill Procedure (Page 3):
  • Bill related to Article 3 shall be introduced in Parliament ONLY with the prior recommendation of the President.
  • Before recommending, the President shall refer the bill to the Legislature of the State concerned for expressing its views.
  • 5th Constitutional Amendment Act (1955): Empowered the President to set a time limit for the State Legislature to express its views (Aur State jo bhi salah de, President ya Parliament use maanne ke liye badhya/bound NAHI hai!).
Article 4 & Article 4(2) Simple Majority (>50%)

Laws Made Under Articles 2 & 3

  • • Article 4(1): Laws made under Article 2 and Article 3 automatically provide for the amendment of the First Schedule (names of States/UTs) and Fourth Schedule (allocation of Rajya Sabha seats).
  • • Article 4(2) — Exam Favourite: Any law made under Article 2 or Article 3 that amends the 1st or 4th Schedule shall NOT be considered a Constitutional Amendment under Article 368!
    👉 Therefore, such a law (creating a new state or changing state name/boundary) can be passed by a Simple Majority (More than 50% present & voting) in Parliament.
⚠️ Exception to Article 3 & 4

What if Indian Territory is Ceded (Given) to a Foreign Country?

• Berubari Union Case (1960): Supreme Court held that Parliament's power to diminish the area of a state (under Art 3) does NOT cover ceding Indian territory to a foreign country. If Indian territory is to be ceded (diya jana hai), then a Constitutional Amendment under Article 368 (Special Majority) is needed!

🇮🇳🇧🇩 100th Constitutional Amendment (2014/2015) Land Boundary Agreement

• Signed for the India-Bangladesh boundary agreement:
👉 India transferred 111 Enclaves to Bangladesh.
👉 Bangladesh transferred 51 Enclaves to India.

Integration of the Princely States (562 + 3 States)

Topics at a Glance
565

In 1947, 562 out of 565 Princely States (Deshi Riyasatein) peacefully agreed to accede to the Indian Union by signing the Instrument of Accession (विलय पत्र) (thanks to Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel & V.P. Menon).
The following 3 Major Princely States initially refused to join India, but were integrated later:

State #1 Oct 1947

1. Jammu & Kashmir

Acceded to India through signing an Instrument of Accession (signed by Maharaja Hari Singh on 26 Oct 1947 after Pakistani tribal invasion).

🔑 Mode: Instrument of Accession
State #2 Feb 1948

2. Junagarh (Gujarat)

Became part of India after conducting a Referendum (जनमत संग्रह) (jahan janta ne vote daal kar Bharat mein shamil hone ka faisla kiya).

🗳️ Mode: Referendum (Plebiscite)
State #3 September 1948

3. Hyderabad

Integrated into India through a military / police action called Operation POLO in September 1948 (against the Nizam's Razakars).

⚔️ Mode: Operation POLO (Sept 1948)

Page 4 • Reorganisation of States

1950 Four-Fold Classification & Linguistic Reorganisation Committees

Topics at a Glance

In 1950: Four-Fold Classification of States (Part A, Part B, Part C, Part D)

Abolished in 1956
Part A States

Included the 9 former Governor's Provinces of British India.

Part B States

Consisted of 9 former Princely States with legislatures (e.g., Hyderabad, Mysore, J&K).

Part C States

Made up of former Chief Commissioner's Provinces of British India and some former Princely States (Total 10).

Part D Territory (Sole)

The Andaman and Nicobar Islands were the sole Part D territory!

Committees on Linguistic Reorganisation of States (Bhasha ke Aadhar par Rajya)

June 1948 ❌ Rejected Language

1. Dhar Committee (1948)

Chairman: S. K. Dhar

• Recommended that states should be reorganized based on administrative convenience (prashasnik suvidha), NOT linguistic factors.

Dec 1948 ❌ Rejected Language

2. JVP Committee (1948)

Members: Jawaharlal Nehru, Vallabhbhai Patel, & Pattabhi Sitaramayya

• It rejected the idea of forming states on a linguistic basis as well (desh ki ekta aur suraksha ko prathamikta di).

🔥 1952 – 1953 Turning Point

1st Linguistic State: Andhra (1953)

• 1952: Death of Congress leader Potti Sriramulu after a 56-day hunger strike for a separate Telugu-speaking state.
• Led to the formation of Andhra Pradesh (1953) (carved out of Madras) — became the 1st Linguistic State of India!

Dec 1953 ✅ Accepted Linguistic Basis

3. Fazl Ali Commission / State Reorganisation Commission (1953)

👥 3 Members (Exam Favourite):
1. Justice Fazl Ali (Chairman)
2. K. M. Panikkar
3. H. N. Kunzru

• Key Verdict: It rejected the theory of "One Language, One State", BUT supported reorganising states on a linguistic basis!

1st November 1956 7th Amendment, 1956

State Reorganisation Act, 1956

  • • Passed based on the recommendations of the Fazl Ali Commission.
  • • It removed the four-fold classification of states (Part A, B, C, D).
  • • Created 14 States + 6 Union Territories on 1st November 1956.

⭐ Sikkim (Special Case)

How Sikkim Joined India (35th vs 36th CA)

  • • Sikkim was earlier an independent kingdom ruled by the Chogyal Dynasty.
  • • 35th Amendment Act, 1974: Sikkim became an "Associate State" of India under Article 2A (and 10th Schedule was added briefly).
  • • 36th Amendment Act, 1975: Gained complete statehood in 1975 (Article 2A repealed) and became the 22nd State of India!

Additional Points & Constitutional Amendments (Page 5)

  • • 10th CA, 1961: Dadra & Nagar Haveli integrated as a UT → Now merged with Daman and Diu (2020).
  • • Operation Vijay, 1961: Launched by Indian Armed Forces to liberate Goa, Daman & Diu from Portuguese rule.
  • • 12th CA, 1962: Goa (UT) + Daman + Diu incorporated as a Union Territory (Goa later became 25th full state in 1987 by 56th CA after being a UT since 1961).
  • • 22nd CA, 1969: Meghalaya was created as an autonomous state within Assam (before becoming full state in 1972).
  • • NEFA Connection: Mizoram and Arunachal Pradesh were created as UTs initially — once part of NEFA (North-East Frontier Agency).
🎉 1st May (Bombay Reorg. Act, 1960):

Bifurcated Bombay into Maharashtra and Gujarat. Both celebrate statehood on 1st May.

🎉 1st November Statehood Day:

Karnataka, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Haryana, and Madhya Pradesh celebrate statehood on 1st November.

Topics at a Glance
Borrowed Feature

Concept of Single Citizenship

Taken from UK (Britain). (USA mein Dual Citizenship hoti hai, par India mein sirf ek — Bharatiya Nagrikta).

Timeline #1 (Page 8)

Before / At Commencement of Constitution (26 Jan 1950)

👉 Governed by Articles 5 to 8

Timeline #2 (Page 8)

After 26th January 1950

👉 Governed by Citizenship Act, 1955 (passed under Art 11)

Article 5 Citizenship at the Commencement of the Constitution

Who became a Citizen of India on 26th January 1950?

A person (having domicile in India) shall be deemed to be a citizen of India on 26 January 1950, if they fulfill ANY ONE of the following 3 conditions:

1. Born in India (Uska janm Bharat mein hua ho); OR
2. Either of the parents was born in India (Mata-Pita mein se kisi ek ka janm Bharat mein hua ho); OR
3. Has been an ordinary resident of India for at least 5 years immediately before 26 January 1950.
Article 6

Migrants from Pakistan to India

Provided rights of citizenship of certain persons who have migrated to India from Pakistan (Partition ke waqt Pakistan se Bharat aane wale log).

🧠 Trick: Art 6 = Pak ➡️ India (Aaye)

Article 7

Migrants to Pakistan (Returned)

Rights of citizenship of certain migrants to Pakistan (India to Pakistan, then came BACK to India for permanent resettlement).

🧠 Trick: Art 7 = India ➡️ Pak ➡️ Back to India

Article 8

Persons of Indian Origin Outside India

Rights of citizenship of certain persons of Indian origin residing outside India (Videsh mein rehne wale Bharatiya mool ke log).

🧠 Trick: Art 8 = Abroad / NRI / PIO

Article 9

Voluntary Acquisition of Foreign Citizenship

Provided that if any person voluntarily acquired the citizenship of a foreign State, they will no longer be a citizen of India.

🧠 Trick: Art 9 = "NO" Dual Citizenship!

Article 10

Continuance of Rights

Continuance of the rights of citizenship (Ek baar nagrik ban gaye toh nagrikta bani rahegi, sivaye Parliament ke kanoon ke).

🧠 Trick: Art 10 = Continue Citizenship

Article 11

Power of Parliament

Parliament to regulate the right of citizenship by law (Isi shakti ka upyog karke Sansad ne Citizenship Act, 1955 banaya).

🧠 Trick: Art 11 = Supreme Power of Parliament

Page 7 • Post-26 Jan 1950 Law

Citizenship Act, 1955: Acquisition (5 Ways) & Loss (3 Ways)

Topics at a Glance
Latin Legal Term #1

1. Jus Soli

👉 Citizenship "By Place of Birth" (Zameen/Mitti ke aadhar par)

Latin Legal Term #2

2. Jus Sanguinis ("By Sanguine")

👉 Citizenship "By Blood / Descent" (Khoon/Vansh ke aadhar par)

5 Ways to Acquire Indian Citizenship (Citizenship Act, 1955)

🧠 Trick: "B - R - A - N - D"
1. By Birth (जन्म से)

Born within the territory of India (currently requires at least one parent to be an Indian citizen and the other not an illegal migrant).

2. By Descent (वंश के आधार पर)

Indian origin, but born/currently living in another country (Parents Indian citizen hain aur bacche ka birth videsh mein hua hai — registered at Indian consulate).

3. By Registration (पंजीकरण द्वारा)

Granted to Indian-origin individuals, spouses of Indian citizens, or minor children who have resided in India for 7 years before applying.

4. By Naturalisation (देशीकरण द्वारा) Total 12 Years (11 + 1)
  • Residing in India for 12 years (11 years in the preceding 14 years + 1 year continuously immediately before application).
  • They must know at least one Indian Language mentioned in the 8th Schedule (22 bhashaon mein se koi ek bhasha aani chahiye).
5. By Incorporating a Territory

When India extends its sovereignty over a new area (e.g., Pondicherry in 1962), the Government declares the people of that territory to be citizens.

3 Ways One Can Lose Indian Citizenship (Nagrikta Kaise Jaati Hai?)

1. Renunciation (स्वैच्छिक त्याग)

When a person voluntarily gives up Indian citizenship by making a declaration.

(Jab koi khud apni marzi se likh kar de de ki mujhe ab Bharat ka nagrik nahi rehna).

2. Termination (बर्खास्तगी / स्वतः समाप्ति)

When an Indian citizen voluntarily acquires the citizenship of another country, their Indian citizenship automatically terminates.

(Dusre desh ka passport lete hi Indian citizenship apne aap khatam).

3. Deprivation (वंचित किया जाना)

Compulsory termination by Govt if citizenship was acquired by fraud, false documents, or during war helped an enemy country.

(Farzi kagaz ya dushman desh ki madad karne par sarkar zabardasti nagrikta chheen leti hai).

1. Six Religious Communities:
1. Hindu
2. Sikh

4. Jain
