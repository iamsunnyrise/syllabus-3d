Supreme Court: Basic Pointers, Article 124 & Collegium System

1. Some Basic Pointers (Part V • Chapter IV: Article 124–147)

Historical Foundation
  • • Integrated & Independent Judiciary: Judiciary in India is Integrated (borrowed from GoI Act of 1935) and Independent.
  • • Replaced Bodies: The Supreme Court replaced the Federal Court and the Judicial Committee of Privy Council after India gained Independence.
  • • 1st Sitting of SC: 28 Jan, 1950
  • • Federal Court Establishment: Federal Court was established in 1937 under the provisions of GoI Act 1935.
  • • Title of Supreme Court: SC is also known as 'The Guardian of the Constitution'.
💡 Hinglish Mein Samjho: Exam mein aksar confusion hota hai ki Integrated Judiciary kahan se liya gaya aur Independent Judiciary / Judicial Review / Removal of Judges kahan se! Dhyan rakho: Integrated Judiciary = GoI Act, 1935 (jiske tahat 1937 mein Federal Court bana tha). Azaadi ke baad 28 January 1950 ko Supreme Court ki pehli baithak hui aur isne Federal Court + Privy Council dono ko replace kar diya!

Article 124 Establishment and Constitution of Supreme Court (Clauses 1 to 7)

Part V • Chapter IV [Art. 124–147]
Clause 1: Strength of Judges

• Originally, strength: 1 Chief Justice + 7 Judges = 8 Judges

• Present, strength: 1 Chief Justice + 33 Judges = 34 Judges

(Maximum sanctioned strength: 34)

Clause 2 & 2(A): Appointment & Tenure

• Appointment (124(2)): Every judge of SC shall be appointed by the President after consultation with CJI.

• Tenure: Serves till the age of 65 years, provided that:

  • (a) a Judge may resign his office by writing to the President.
  • (b) a Judge to be removed in the manner provided in clause (4).

• Clause 2(A): The age of a SC Judge to be determined in such manner as Parliament may by law provide (15th CAA, 1963).

Clause 3: Qualification for SC Judges

Must be a Citizen of India + ANY ONE of:

  • (a) At least 5 years as a Judge of a High Court, OR
  • (b) At least 10 years as an Advocate of a High Court, OR
  • (c) Distinguished jurist in the opinion of the President.
Clause 4 & 5: Removal of SC Judges (Brought from USA) Judges Enquiry Act, 1968

• Clause 4: A Judge can be removed by President after Parliament passes an address by Special Majority (i.e. majority of total membership + 2/3rd of members present and voting) on grounds of: Proved misbehaviour or Incapacity.

• Statutory Procedure: Procedure of Removal for Supreme Court/High Court Judges is mentioned in Judges Enquiry Act, 1968. Motion to remove judges should be signed by 50 members of Rajya Sabha or 100 members of Lok Sabha.

• Clause 5: Parliament may by law establish procedure for investigating judge's misconduct as mentioned in clause (4).

Clause 6 & 7: Oath & Restrictions

• Clause 6 (Oath): A Judge of SC to subscribe an Oath or affirmation before the President.

• Clause 7 (Restrictions): After retirement, a SC judge cannot practice law in any court within the territory of India.

💡 Hinglish Mein Samjho: SC Judge ke liye minimum age Constitution mein kahin nahi di gayi hai, sirf maximum retirement age 65 years hai! Qualification ka formula yaad rakho: 5 साल Judge + 10 साल Advocate + Distinguished Jurist. Aur hatane (Removal) ke liye Lok Sabha mein 100 MPs ya Rajya Sabha mein 50 MPs ke sign chahiye under Judges Enquiry Act, 1968. Aaj tak ek bhi SC Judge remove nahi hua hai!

3. Collegium System & The Four Judges Cases

⚠️ Not Mentioned in Constitution
1st Judges Case (1982)
S.P. Gupta Case
  • Recommendation of CJI not binding on the President.
  • Gave primacy to the executive in judicial appointments.
2nd Judges Case (1993)
Birth of Collegium
  • Introduced the Collegium system, holding that "Consultation" means Concurrence.
  • Recommendation made by CJI + 2 senior-most judges.
  • Recommendation given by CJI will be binding on President.
3rd Judges Case (1998)
5-Member Expansion
  • Collegium expanded to a 5 member body.
  • Composition: CJI + 4 senior-most judges.
  • Decision binding on the President.
99th CA, 2014
NJAC Introduced

The Parliament replaced the collegium system by National Judicial Appointments Commission (NJAC) through 99th CA, 2014 to provide recommendations to the President.

4th Judges Case (2015)
NJAC Struck Down
  • 99th CA was declared unconstitutional and NJAC Act was struck down.
  • Collegium System was restored.
🧠 Trick to Remember (Judges Cases Timeline — 1982 → 1993 → 1998 → 2015):
• 1982 (1st Case - S.P. Gupta): Executive boss tha (Consultation ≠ Concurrence).
• 1993 (2nd Case): Collegium aaya (1 CJI + 2 Judges = 3) aur Consultation = Concurrence ban gaya.
• 1998 (3rd Case): Collegium bada hua (1 CJI + 4 Judges = 5).
• 2014–2015 (99th CA & 4th Case): Parliament ne 99th CA (2014) se NJAC banaya, par 2015 (4th Case) mein SC ne NJAC ko khatam karke wapas Collegium chalu kar diya!

Article 125
Salaries, etc., of Judges

• Salaries & allowances are determined by Parliament.

Article 126
Appointment of Acting Chief Justice

President appoints Acting Chief Justice when:

  • Chief Justice's seat is vacant, or
  • Chief Justice is unable to perform duties.
Article 128
Attendance of Retired Judges

• Retired SC Judges can be invited to sit and act as Judge of SC.

Article 127
Appointment of Ad Hoc Judges (Temporary Judges)
  • CJI, with prior consent of the President and in consultation with the Chief Justice of the concerned High Court, may request a High Court judge (qualified for SC) to sit as an ad hoc judge (temporary judge) in the Supreme Court.
  • Such ad hoc judge enjoys powers, jurisdiction, and privileges of SC judges during the time.
Article 129
Supreme Court as a Court of Record
  • Its decisions are preserved as legal precedents.
  • Has power to punish for 'Contempt of court' (Not mentioned in Constitution).
Article 130
Seat of Supreme Court
  • The seat of the Supreme Court is at Delhi.
  • SC can sit elsewhere if CJI recommends and President approves.
Article 131
Original Jurisdiction of Supreme Court
✅ Exclusive Original Jurisdiction in disputes:
  • Between Government of India and one or more States.
  • Between GoI and any state or states on one side and one or more states on the other side.
  • Between two or more States.
❌ Exceptions (NOT under Art. 131):
  • Dispute arising of Pre-Constitution Treaty
  • Inter State Water Dispute
  • Matters (Manners) related to Finance Commission.

1) Pre-constitutional matters (SC must give its advice to President)
2) Matters of General importance

💡 Hinglish Mein Samjho (Super Exam Points):
1. Art. 127 (Ad Hoc Judges): Sirf Supreme Court mein hote hain (High Court mein Additional & Acting Judges hote hain, Art. 224). CJI unhe President ki prior consent aur concerned HC ke Chief Justice se consult karke bulate hain.
2. Art. 132, 133, 134 (Appeals): Line se yaad rakho — 132 = Constitutional, 133 = Civil (Property, Marriage, Divorce), 134 = Criminal.
3. Art. 143 (Advisory Jurisdiction): President SC se salah maang sakta hai. Pre-constitutional treaty/matter par SC ko salah dena compulsory (must) hai, par general importance ke matter par SC mana bhi kar sakta hai. Dono hi cases mein SC ki salah President par binding NAHI hoti!

Constitution of High Courts

• One Chief Justice and other Judges as Parliament may decide.

Application of Certain Provisions

• Applies SC Judges' removal procedure to HC Judges (Judges Inquiry Act, 1968).

Oath or Affirmation

• HC judge takes oath before the Governor.

Article 217
Appointment and Conditions of HC Judges

1. Appointment: Done by the President after consultation with:

  • Chief Justice of India
  • Governor of State
  • Chief Justice of the High Court (Collegium)

• Maximum Age: 62 years

• Resignation: Handed to the President.

• Removal: Same as that of SC Judge.

2. Qualifications for HC Judge:
  • 10 years advocate of HC, OR
  • 10 years holding judicial office.
Article 220
Restriction on Practice After Retirement

• Former HC judges cannot practice law before any court except: SC & other HCs.

Article 222
Transfer of Judges

• President can transfer HC judges after consultation with CJI.

Article 221 • 🔥 Exam Trap
Salaries and Allowances of HC Judges
  • Salaries & allowances: Drawn from Consolidated Fund of State.
  • Pensions: Drawn from Consolidated Fund of India.
  • Salaries, allowances, leaves and pension rights are determined by the Parliament.
Article 223
Appointment of Acting Chief Justice (HC)

• President can appoint an Acting Chief Justice when: (a) office of CJ of HC is vacant, or (b) CJ is unable to perform duties due to absence or any other reason.

Article 224A
Retired Judges at Sittings of HC

• The Chief Justice of a HC can request the attendance of a retired judge of that HC or any other HC.

Article 224
Additional and Acting Judges
  • (1) Additional Judges: If workload of HC increases (e.g. due to arrears), the President may appoint additional judges for a temporary period not exceeding 2 years.
  • (2) Acting Judges: If a judge is absent, unable to perform duties, or seat is vacant, the President may appoint an acting judge.
  • Both appointments are done in consultation with CJI.
💡 Hinglish Mein Samjho (High Court Traps):
• Oath vs Resignation/Removal: HC Judge ko Oath (Art. 219) Governor dilata hai, lekin HC Judge apna Appointment, Resignation aur Removal sab President ke haatho hota hai!
• Salary vs Pension (Art. 221): HC Judge state mein kaam karta hai isliye uski Salary → Consolidated Fund of State se aati hai, par retire hone ke baad Pension → Consolidated Fund of India se di jaati hai!

HC Jurisdiction, Writs (Art. 226), Common HCs & Historical Firsts

Article 225
Jurisdiction of High Court
  • Original Jurisdiction: Revenue matters, election disputes of MPs and MLAs.
  • Appellate Jurisdiction: Hear appeals about decisions made by lower courts.
  • Writ jurisdiction.
Article 226 • ⭐ V. Imp
Power of HC to Issue Writs
  • HC has wider writ jurisdiction than SC.
  • It can issue writs on Legal + Fundamental Rights.
  • HC may refuse to issue writ, SC cannot (under Art. 32).
Chandra Kumar Case, 1997
Judicial Review = Basic Feature

The power of Judicial Review held by the High Courts (under Articles 226 and 227) is a basic feature of the Constitution and cannot be excluded by Parliament through the creation of tribunals.

Lok Adalats & National Legal Services Authority (NALSA)

Concept & Statutory Status
What is a Lok Adalat?
  • It is one of the alternative dispute redressal (ADR) mechanisms.
  • It is a forum where disputes/cases pending in the court of law or at the pre-litigation stage are settled or compromised amicably.
  • Statutory Status: Lok Adalats have been given statutory status under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987.
  • Court Fees: There is no court fee payable when a matter is filed in a Lok Adalat.
Conducting Body: NALSA
Who Conducts Lok Adalats?

NALSA (National Legal Services Authority), along with other legal services institutions.

👑 Patron Chief (NALSA): Chief Justice of India (CJI)
⚖️ Executive Chief (NALSA): Seniormost Judge of Supreme Court
Jurisdiction & Scope
Nature of Cases Referred to Lok Adalats
  • Any case pending before any court.
  • Any dispute that has not been brought before any court and is likely to be filed before the court (pre-litigation).
🚫 Non-Compoundable offences cannot be dealt by Lok Adalats.
Award (Decision) & Appeal Rules in Lok Adalats
Final & Binding
1. Status of Award & No Appeal Rule: The award (Decision) made by the Lok Adalats is deemed to be a decree of a civil court and is final and binding on all parties, and no appeal against such an award lies before any court of law.
2. What if Parties are NOT Satisfied? If the parties are not satisfied with the award of the Lok Adalats, though there is no provision for an appeal against such an award, they are free to Initiate Litigation by approaching the court of appropriate jurisdiction by filing a case by following the required procedure, in exercise of their right to litigate.
💡 Hinglish Mein Samjho (Lok Adalat Most Asked MCQs):
• Lok Adalat ka faisla Civil Court ki decree ke barabar maana jata hai aur iske khilaaf kisi bhi court mein Appeal NAHI ho sakti (kyunki faisla dono parties ki aapsi sahmati/compromise se hota hai).
• Lekin agar parties संतुष्ट (satisfied) nahi hain, toh wo appeal toh nahi kar sakti, par apne Right to Litigate ka istemal karke normal court mein naya case file (Initiate Litigation) kar sakti hain!
• Non-compoundable offences (gambhir apradh jinmein samjhauta kanoonan mana hai) Lok Adalat mein solve NAHI ho sakte.
