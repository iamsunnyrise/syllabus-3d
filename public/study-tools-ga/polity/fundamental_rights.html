Pages 2, 3 & 4 • Foundation of Part III

Introduction, Salient Features & Articles 12 – 13

Topics at a Glance
Basic Pointers (Page 2) Part III: Articles 12 to 35

📌 Definition: Fundamental Rights are a set of legally enforceable rights guaranteed by the Constitution.

  • Borrowed From: Constitution of USA (Bill of Rights).
  • Nicknames in Exam: Termed as "Conscience of the Constitution" and referred to as the "Magna Carta of India" (Part III).
  • Main Aim: They seek to implement Political Democracy in India (while DPSPs implement Social & Economic Democracy).
  • ⭐ 7 to 6 Fundamental Rights (Right to Property Removed): Originally there were 7 Fundamental Rights, currently there are 6 Fundamental Rights.
    • Right to Property (Art 31 & Art 19(1)(f)) was removed from Part III in 1978 by 44th Constitutional Amendment Act.
    • It is now a Legal / Constitutional Right under Article 300-A in Part XII (Part XII relates to Finance, Property, Contracts and Suits).
Salient Features of Fundamental Rights
  • • Not Absolute: They are NOT absolute, but qualified (State can impose reasonable restrictions).
  • • Not Sacrosanct or Permanent: Parliament can curtail or repeal them ONLY by a Constitutional Amendment Act (without destroying the Basic Structure).
  • • Suspension During Emergency: They can be curtailed/suspended during National Emergency — EXCEPT Articles 20 and 21, which can NEVER be suspended!
  • • Justiciable in Nature: Agar inka violation ho toh vyakti seedhe Supreme Court (Art 32) ya High Court (Art 226) ja sakta hai.

Six Fundamental Rights in the Constitution:

1. Right to Equality Art 14–18
2. Right to Freedom Art 19–22
3. Against Exploitation Art 23–24
4. Freedom of Religion Art 25–28
5. Cultural & Educational Art 29–30
6. Constitutional Remedies Art 32
Article 12 Who is "State"?

Definition of "State" (राज्य की परिभाषा)

It defines the term "State" for Part III, which includes:

  • The Government and Parliament of India (Union Executive & Legislative organs).
  • The Government and Legislature of each of the States.
  • All local authorities (Municipalities, Panchayats, District Boards).
  • All other authorities (statutory or non-statutory) within the territory of India or under control of Govt of India.
🏢 Examples of "State" under Art 12: PSUs, Municipalities, Legislative bodies, LIC, ONGC, SAIL, BHEL, etc.
Article 13 Judicial Review Shield

Laws Inconsistent with or in Derogation of Fundamental Rights

  • Null & Void (Judicial Review): Any law made before or after the commencement of the Constitution shall be declared null and void by the Judiciary if it violates Fundamental Rights!
  • What does "Law" include? Any ordinance, order, bye-law, rule, regulation, notification, custom or usage having the force of law in the territory of India.
  • Important Exception — Art 13(4) (24th CA, 1971): This article shall NOT apply to any Constitutional Amendment made under Article 368 (However, in Kesavananda Bharati Case 1973, SC held that even an Art 368 Amendment can be struck down if it violates the Basic Structure).
🥊 Hinglish Visual: Article 13 Fundamental Rights ka Bodyguard hai — koi bhi kanoon agar FRs ko chot pahuchayega toh Supreme Court/High Court use turant radd (null & void) kar dega!

Pages 3, 4 & 5 • Right to Equality (Articles 14 – 18)

Articles 14 & 15 and Champakam Dorairajan Case (1951)

Topics at a Glance
Article 14

Equality Before Law & Equal Protection of Laws

⚠️ Exceptions to Art 14: President, Governors, MPs/MLAs, Foreign Dignitaries/Ambassadors enjoy immunity from certain civil/criminal arrests.

1. Equality Before Law (कानून के समक्ष समता)

🇬🇧 British Concept
  • • Nature: It is Negative in nature (kyunki yeh State ko kisi ko special privilege dene se rokta hai).
  • • Core Meaning: No one is above the law & absence of any special privilege in favour of any person.
  • • Rule of Law: This concept is an element of the "Rule of Law" given by A. V. Dicey (famous British Jurist).

2. Equal Protection of Laws (कानूनों का समान संरक्षण)

🇺🇸 American Concept
  • • Nature: It is Positive in nature (Equity based).
  • • Core Meaning: Equal treatment under equal circumstances ("Likes should be treated alike" — Saman paristhiti mein saman vyavahar).
  • • Hinglish Example: Ek ameer vyakti aur ek gareeb vyakti par barabar tax nahi lagaya ja sakta; alag-alag halat walo ke liye alag niyam banana hi Equity / Equal Protection hai!
Article 15 (Citizens Only) 5 Grounds Only: R-R-C-S-P

Prohibition of Discrimination on Grounds of Religion, Race, Caste, Sex or Place of Birth

Article 15(1): The State shall not discriminate against any citizen on grounds ONLY of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth (5 grounds).
Article 15(2) [Applies to State + Private Individuals]: No citizen shall, on these 5 grounds, be subject to any restriction with regard to:
• (a) Access to shops, public restaurants, hotels and places of public entertainment; OR
• (b) Use of wells, tanks, bathing ghats, roads and places of public resort maintained wholly or partly out of State funds.
Article 15(3) [Exception 1]: The State can make special provisions for Women and Children (e.g., Metro/Bus seats reserved for women).
Article 15(4) [Exception 2 — Added by 1st CA, 1951]: The State can make special provision for the advancement of socially & educationally backward classes, Scheduled Castes (SCs) and Scheduled Tribes (STs).
⚖️ Landmark Case (Page 5)

Champakam Dorairajan Case (1951)

(Led to the very 1st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1951!)

  • Background: Champakam Dorairajan, a woman from Madras, challenged the Madras government's Communal General Order (which gave caste-based reservation in medical/engineering colleges) in Madras High Court citing violation of her Right to Equality.
  • Supreme Court Verdict (1951): A 5-judge SC bench upheld the Madras HC ruling, declaring the Communal G.O. unconstitutional because it violated Article 14 and Article 15(1) (and Art 29(2)).
  • Historic Principle Established: The SC ruled that Fundamental Rights prevail over DPSPs (Directive Principles) in case of conflict, and established that Parliament can amend FRs through Constitutional Amendments (hence Nehru Govt passed 1st CA 1951 to add Article 15(4)!).

Pages 5 & 6 • Right to Equality (Continued)

Articles 16 (Public Employment & Mandal Case), 17 (Untouchability) & 18 (Titles)

Topics at a Glance
Article 16 (Citizens Only) 7 Grounds

Equality of Opportunity in Matters of Public Employment

Art 16(1): Equality of opportunity for all citizens in matters relating to employment or appointment to any office under the State.
Art 16(2) [7 Grounds]: No citizen shall be discriminated against in State employment on grounds only of: Religion, Race, Caste, Sex, Place of Birth + Descent (वंश) and Residence (निवास).
Art 16(3) [Residence Exception]: Parliament/State can set rules giving preference to people who are residents of that area/State for local employment or appointments.
Art 16(4) [Reservation in Jobs]: State can make rules to reserve jobs or positions for any "backward class of citizens" who are not adequately represented in the State services.

Commissions & Cases Related to Article 16(4):

Year: 1953
1. Kalelkar Commission (1953)
  • • 1st Backward Classes Commission (appointed under Art 340).
  • • Chairman: Kaka Kalelkar.
Year: 1979
2. Mandal Commission (1979)
  • • 2nd Backward Classes Commission (Chairman: B. P. Mandal).
  • • Recommended 27% reservation for OBCs.
  • • Implemented by PM V. P. Singh in 1990.
Indra Sawhney Case (1992) Also known as "Mandal Case"
  • Public Interest Litigation (PIL) filed by Advocate Indra Sawhney.
  • Supreme Court upheld the 27% reservation for OBCs in government jobs, BUT set a 50% cap (ceiling limit) on total reservations.
  • Directed that "Creamy Layer" (socially/economically advanced section) in OBCs must be identified and is NOT entitled to reservation.
💡 DID YOU KNOW? Public Interest Litigation (PIL) is taken from: 🇺🇸 USA's Constitution
Article 17 Absolute Right

Abolition of Untouchability (अस्पृश्यता का अंत)

  • Both the State and any private individual are strictly prohibited from practicing untouchability in any form.
  • ⭐ Exam Trap Fact: The term 'Untouchability' has NOWHERE been defined in the Constitution (nor in the Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955).
Article 18 No Maharaja / Rai Bahadur

Abolition of Titles (उपाधियों का अंत)

  • No title, other than a military or academic distinction (e.g., Param Vir Chakra, Doctor, Professor), shall be conferred by the State (Abolished colonial titles like Maharaja, Rai Bahadur, Dewan Bahadur).
  • No citizen of India shall accept any title from any foreign State.
  • No person holding any office of profit under the State shall receive any "Title" from any foreign State without the consent of the President.

Page 6 • Right to Freedom (Articles 19 – 22)

Article 19: Six Democratic Freedoms ("SAAMRP"), Cases & Restrictions

Topics at a Glance
🧠 Memory Trick for 6 Freedoms under Article 19(1)

"S - A - A - M - R - P"

1. Speech (19a) → 2. Assemble (19b) → 3. Association/Co-op (19c) → 4. Movement (19d) → 5. Reside (19e) → [19f Property Omitted] → 6. Profession (19g)!

97th CA (2011): Added Co-op Societies in 19(1)(c)
19(1)(a) S = Speech

To freedom of speech and expression.

19(1)(b) A = Assemble

To assemble peaceably and without arms (Bina hathiyar shantipurna sabha).

19(1)(c) A = Association + Co-op (97th CA)

To form associations or unions or cooperative societies. (Note: 97th Amendment, 2011 added Right to form Cooperative Societies under Art 19(1)(c)).

19(1)(d) M = Move

To move freely throughout the territory of India.

19(1)(e) R = Reside

To reside and settle in any part of the territory of India.

19(1)(f) ❌ Omitted

Right to Property — Omitted by 44th CA, 1978 (now Art 300-A).

19(1)(g) P = Profession

To practise any profession, or to carry on any occupation, trade or business.

⚖️ Landmark Cases & Hidden Rights (Art 19)
1. Romesh Thappar vs. State of Madras (1950): Held that Freedom of Press is an integral part of Right to Speech and Expression under Art 19(1)(a).
2. Shreya Singhal vs. Union of India (2015): Supreme Court struck down / repealed Section 66-A of the IT Act (which criminalized 'offensive' online messages) as violative of Art 19(1)(a).
🔍 Hidden / Implied Rights under Article 19:
  • • Right to Internet: Anuradha Bhasin vs. Union of India Case, 2020 (J&K internet shutdown case).
  • • Right to Information (RTI): State of UP vs. Raj Narain Case, 1975.
Fundamental Right under Art 19(1) Restriction Clause Permissible Grounds of Reasonable Restrictions
1. Speech & Expression [19(1)(a)] Article 19(2) Sovereignty & integrity of India, security of the State, friendly relations with foreign States, public order, decency or morality, contempt of court, defamation, incitement to an offence.
2. Peaceful Assembly [19(1)(b)] Article 19(3) Sovereignty and integrity of India, public order.
3. Association / Unions [19(1)(c)] Article 19(4) Sovereignty and integrity of India, public order, morality.
4. Movement & Residence [19(1)(d) & (e)] Article 19(5) In the interests of the general public, or for the protection of the interests of any Scheduled Tribe (ST).
5. Profession / Trade [19(1)(g)] Article 19(6) In the interests of the general public, prescribing professional/technical qualifications, or State monopoly over any trade/business.

Page 7 • Non-Suspendable Rights (Never Suspended in Emergency)

Article 20 (Conviction Shields) & Article 21 (Life, Liberty & 7 Landmark Cases)

Topics at a Glance
Article 20

Protection in Respect of Conviction for Offences (अपराधों के लिए दोषसिद्धि के संबंध में संरक्षण)

🛡️ Never Suspended During Emergency
Clause (1)

1. No Ex-Post-Facto Law

No person shall be convicted of any offence except for violation of a law in force at the time of the crime, nor subjected to a penalty greater than that prescribed by law when the crime was committed.

💡 Aaj banaya gaya criminal kanoon pichhle saal ke jurm par laagu nahi hoga! (Applies only to Criminal laws, not Civil/Tax laws).

Clause (2)

2. No Double Jeopardy

A person must NOT be prosecuted and punished twice for the same offence (Ek hi jurm ke liye do baar saza nahi di ja sakti).

💡 Hindi mein: दोहरे दंड से सुरक्षा.

Clause (3)

3. No Self-Incrimination

A person accused of an offence cannot be compelled to provide information or testify against oneself (Apne hi khilaf gawahi dene ke liye majboor nahi kiya ja sakta).

⚠️ Exception: Accused HAS to submit blood specimens, thumb impressions, signatures if asked!
Article 21 • Foremost Right

Protection of Life and Personal Liberty (प्राण और दैहिक स्वतंत्रता)

"No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law."

⭐ The foremost right among Right to Freedom is Right to Life and Personal Liberty (Art 21).

🇯🇵 Japanese Constitution

1. Procedure Established by Law

Explicitly written in Article 21. Checks only whether the law was passed by following the correct legislative procedure.

Upheld in A.K. Gopalan Case (1950)
🇺🇸 American Concept (USA)

2. Due Process of Law

Ensures fair, just, and reasonable legal proceedings (not explicitly mentioned in the Constitution text, but introduced by SC in 1978!).

Introduced in Maneka Gandhi Case (1978)

All 7 Landmark Cases & Interpretations under Article 21 (Page 7)

1950 • Narrow View

1. A. K. Gopalan Case (1950)

The Supreme Court took a narrow view of Article 21 and ruled that it does NOT guarantee American "Due Process of Law" (protection only against arbitrary executive action, not legislative action).

1978 • Wide View (Turning Point) Passport Impounding Case

2. Maneka Gandhi vs. Union of India Case (1978)

  • Overturned the Gopalan case ruling, took a wide stance on Article 21, and introduced "Due Process of Law".
  • Made Right to Travel Abroad a Fundamental Right under Personal Liberty (Art 21).
  • Included implied rights under Art 21: Right to Health, Shelter, Speedy Trial, Information, Sleep, Electricity, Privacy, etc.
1979 • Bihar Undertrials

3. Hussainara Khatoon vs. State of Bihar (1979)

Established the need for a 'reasonable, fair and just' procedure, including Free Legal Services for the poor and the Right to Speedy Trial.

Death Penalty Doctrine

4. Bachan Singh vs. State of Punjab Case

Introduced the famous principle that the Death Penalty (Capital Punishment) should be applied only in the "Rarest of Rare" (जघन्यतम) cases.

1985 • Right to Livelihood

5. Olga Tellis vs. Bombay Municipal Corp. (1985)

Eviction of pavement dwellers using unreasonable force without giving them a chance to explain is unconstitutional and a violation of the Right to Livelihood (आजीविका का अधिकार) under Art 21.

2011 Judgment • Right to Die with Dignity

6. Aruna Shanbaug vs. Union of India (2011)

The Supreme Court in a 2011 judgment legalized Passive Euthanasia (निष्क्रिय इच्छामृत्यु) under strict conditions (withholding or withdrawing life support in specific circumstances), while rejecting Active Euthanasia.

2017 • Right to Privacy

7. Justice K. S. Puttaswamy Case

Affirmed that the Right to Privacy (निजता का अधिकार) is an intrinsic part of the Right to Life and Personal Liberty under Article 21.

Page 8 • Education, Arrest Safeguards & Exploitation

Article 21(A) (RTE), Article 22 (Detention) & Articles 23–24 (Exploitation)

Topics at a Glance
⭐ Super Important Amendment

Article 21(A): Right to Education (शिक्षा का अधिकार)

• Added by the 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002.
• Guarantees the right to free and compulsory education for all children between the ages of 6 and 14 years.

Age Group Covered 6 to 14 Years 86th CAA, 2002
Article 22

Protection Against Arrest and Detention in Certain Cases

1. Punitive Detention & Ordinary Arrest

After Crime

• Punitive Detention: Occurs after a crime has been committed.

A person arrested under Ordinary Law has 4 Rights:

  • Right to be informed of the grounds of arrest.
  • Right to consult and be defended by a legal practitioner (lawyer).
  • Right to be produced before a Magistrate within 24 hours (excluding journey time).
  • Right to be released after 24 hours unless the Magistrate authorises further detention.

2. Preventive Detention (निवारक निरोध)

Max 3 Months

• Preventive Detention: Detention without trial to prevent a possible future offence (Shaq ke aadhar par pehle hi rokna).

  • This detention cannot exceed 3 months, unless an Advisory Board of High Court Judges approves an extension!
  • Detenu must be informed of the grounds of detention (except facts against public interest).
  • Should be given the earliest opportunity to make a representation against the detention order.

3. Right Against Exploitation (शोषण के विरुद्ध अधिकार — Articles 23 & 24)

Article 23 Human Trafficking & Begar

Prohibition of Traffic in Human Beings and Forced Labour

• Prohibits trafficking in human beings (insano ki kharid-bikri) and Forced Labour (Begar / बेगार — bina paisa diye zabardasti kaam karwana).

⚠️ Exception [Art 23(2)]: The State can force people to do compulsory service for public purposes (for e.g., military conscription during a War), without discriminating on religion, race, caste, or class.
Article 24 Below 14 Years

Prohibition of Employment of Children in Factories, etc.

  • No child below the age of 14 years shall be employed to work in any Factory, Mine, or engaged in any other hazardous occupations.
  • They can, however, work at safe/non-hazardous places (family enterprises) during non-school hours.
🧠 Quick Confusion Buster:
• Article 23 = Bado/Sabhi ka शोषण (Human Trafficking & Begar)
• Article 24 = 14 saal se chhote Baccho ka शोषण (24 = 14 years)!

Pages 8 & 9 • Religion & Minority Rights

Right to Freedom of Religion (Art 25–28) & Cultural Rights (Art 29–30)

Topics at a Glance

4. Right to Freedom of Religion (धार्मिक स्वतंत्रता का अधिकार — Articles 25 to 28)

Article 25 Individual Freedom

Freedom of Conscience and Free Profession, Practice and Propagation of Religion

Har vyakti ko apne dharm ko maanne (Profession), uske riti-riwaz nibhane (Practice), aur uska prachar-prasar karne (Propagation) ki azaadi hai.

⚠️ Important Point: Right to propagate religion prohibits forcible conversion (Zabardasti dharm parivartan karwana allowed NAHI hai)!
Article 26 Collective / Group Right

Freedom to Manage Religious Affairs

Every religious denomination / institution or section shall have the right to:

  • (a) Establish and maintain religious & charitable institutions.
  • (b) Manage their own religious affairs.
  • (c) Own and acquire movable and immovable property.
  • (d) Administer such property in accordance with law.
Article 27 Tax ❌ vs Fee ✅

Freedom as to Payment of Taxes for Promotion of Any Particular Religion

Kisi bhi vyakti ko kisi ek vishesh dharm ke prachar ya rakh-rakhav ke liye tax dene par majboor nahi kiya jayega.

⭐ SSC Favourite Trap: This provision prohibits ONLY the levy of TAXES and NOT a FEE! (Sarkar teerth-yatriyo ki suvidha/safety ke liye 'Fee' le sakti hai, par 'Tax' nahi).
Article 28 4 Types of Institutions

Freedom from Attending Religious Instruction in Schools

No religious instruction shall be provided in any educational institution wholly funded by the State:

(a) Completely maintained by State:
❌ Completely Prohibited
(b) Administered by State but established under Trust:
✅ Permitted
(c) Recognised by State:
⚠️ Permitted on Voluntary Basis
(d) Receiving Aid from State:
⚠️ Permitted on Voluntary Basis

5. Cultural and Educational Rights (संस्कृति और शिक्षा संबंधी अधिकार — Articles 29 & 30)

Article 29

Protection of Interests of Minorities

• Any section of citizens residing in India having a distinct language, script, or culture (भाषा, लिपि या संस्कृति) of its own has the right to conserve it.

Article 30

Right of Minorities to Establish & Administer Educational Institutions

• All minorities (religious or linguistic) have the right to establish and administer educational institutions, and the State cannot discriminate based on religion or language while providing aid.

💡 DID YOU KNOW? (Page 9)
  • Article 29 is NOT solely specific to minorities (applies to "any section of citizens", i.e., both majority & minorities), whereas Article 30 is specifically for minorities!
  • The term 'Minority' is NOWHERE defined in the Constitution!

Article 32: Right to Constitutional Remedies & All 5 Writs

Topics at a Glance
6th Fundamental Right • Article 32

Known as the "Heart and Soul of the Constitution" — Dr. B. R. Ambedkar

  • 1. Right to Approach SC: Guarantees the right to move the Supreme Court directly for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights.
  • 2. Supreme Court's Power: The SC can issue orders, directions, or 5 types of Writs to enforce Fundamental Rights.
  • 3. Power of Parliament [Art 32(3)]: Parliament can authorise any other court (other than SC & HC) to issue writs within its local limits.

The 5 Prerogative Writs Issued Under Article 32 (SC) & Article 226 (HC)

Writ #1 "To have the body of"

1. Habeas Corpus (बंदी प्रत्यक्षीकरण)

  • Literal Meaning: "To have the body of" (Bandi ko sashareer pesh kiya jaye).
  • Purpose: It ensures individual liberty against unlawful detention (Issued against both Public Authorities AND Private Individuals).
  • Famous Case: ADM Jabalpur Case (1976) is also known as the Habeas Corpus Case, related to 'Personal Liberty' during Emergency.
Writ #2 "We Command"

2. Mandamus (परमादेश)

  • Literal Meaning: "We Command" (Hum aadesh dete hain).
  • Purpose: Orders a public official or public authority to perform a mandatory public duty that they failed or refused to do.
  • ❌ Cannot be issued against: A private individual, President, Governor, or when the duty is discretionary (not mandatory).
Writ #3 • Preventive "To Forbid"

3. Prohibition (प्रतिषेध)

  • Literal Meaning: "To Forbid" (Rokna / Mana karna).
  • Nature: It is Preventive in nature (Faisla aane se pehle hi rokna).
  • Purpose: Issued by a superior court to a lower court/tribunal to prevent the latter from exceeding its jurisdiction.
  • ❌ Not available against: Administrative bodies, legislative bodies, and private individuals.
Writ #4 • Curative "To be Certified"

4. Certiorari (उत्प्रेषण)

  • Literal Meaning: "To be certified" (or to be informed).
  • Nature: It is Curative in nature (Faisla aane ke baad use quash/radd karna).
  • Purpose: Issued by a higher court to transfer a case pending in a lower court to itself OR nullify/quash the lower court's order if it acted beyond jurisdiction.
  • ❌ Exceptions: Cannot be issued against Legislative Bodies and Private Individuals.
Writ #5 "By what authority"

5. Quo-Warranto (अधिकार-पृच्छा)

  • Literal Meaning: "By what authority or warrant" (Aap kis adhikar se is kursi par baithe hain?).
  • Purpose: Challenges a person's legal right to hold a public office (prevents illegal usurpation of a public post).

Page 11 • Final Comparison & Exceptions

SC (Art 32) vs HC (Art 226) Writs, Articles 33–35 & Citizens-Only Rights

Topics at a Glance

🏛️ Supreme Court (Article 32)

Mandatory Duty
  • Cannot Refuse: The SC cannot refuse to issue a writ (because Article 32 is itself a Fundamental Right!).
  • Scope of Writs (Narrower): It can issue writs ONLY when there is a violation of Fundamental Rights.
  • Territorial Jurisdiction (Wider): Throughout the territory of India.

⚖️ High Court (Article 226)

Wider Writ Scope!
  • Can Refuse (Discretionary): The HC can refuse to issue a writ (Art 226 is a constitutional right, not a Fundamental Right).
  • Scope of Writs (Wider): It can issue writs for violation of Fundamental Rights + Ordinary Legal Rights (hence, writ jurisdiction of HC is WIDER than SC!).
  • Territorial Jurisdiction: Throughout the State (within its territorial limits).
Article 33 50th CA, 1984

Armed Forces, Police & Intelligence

Empowers Parliament to restrict or abrogate the Fundamental Rights of the members of Armed Forces, Police, and Intelligence Agencies to ensure proper discipline and discharge of duty. (50th Amendment, 1984).

Article 34 Borrowed: UK

Martial Law (सैन्य शासन)

Provides for restrictions on Fundamental Rights while Martial Law (Military Rule) is enforced in any area within India.
• Concept of Martial Law is borrowed from: UK (Britain).

Article 35 Only Parliament

Exclusive Power of Parliament

Vests exclusive powers in Parliament (and NOT in State Legislatures) to make laws relating to enforcement of certain Fundamental Rights (Art 16(3), 32(3), 33, 34, and punishments under Art 17 & 23).

Baaki saare Fundamental Rights (14, 20, 21, 21A, 22, 23, 24, 25–28) Bharatiya nagriko aur Videshiyo dono ko milte hain, lekin yeh 5 Articles SIRF Indian Citizens ke liye hain:
