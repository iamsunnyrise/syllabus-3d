President of India: Article (52–62)
Article 52

There shall be a President of India, who shall be the Head of the Union Executive.

Article 53
  • Executive Power of Union shall vest in President.
  • He is the Supreme Commander of all the Defence forces (Army, Navy, Airforce).
The President is:
  • Head of the Country
  • First Citizen of India
  • Nominal Head / Titular Head / De jure head of the Country

Article 54 Election of President (Electoral College)

✅ WHO PARTICIPATES (Electoral College):
  • Elected members of both Houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha)
  • Elected Members of the Legislative Assemblies (MLAs) of States + Union Territories of Delhi and Puducherry
❌ WHO DOES NOT PARTICIPATE:
  • Nominated Members of Parliament (LS & RS)
  • Members of Legislative Council (MLCs) (both Elected & Nominated)
  • Nominated MLAs of States/UTs

Article 55 Manner of Election & Vote Value Calculations

• Indirect Election (not directly by the people) • Proportional Representation + Single Transferable Vote (STV) • Voting done by Secret Ballot
1. Value of Vote of an MLA
(Total Population of the State / Total Number of Elected MLAs) × (1 / 1000)
2. Value of Vote of an MP
Total Value of Votes of all MLAs / Total Number of Elected MPs
3. Electoral Quota
[ Total Number of Valid Votes Polled / (Vacancies + 1) ] + 1

President: Term, Qualification, Oath, Impeachment & Vacancy (Art 56–62)

🧠 Master Mnemonic Trick (Art 52 to 62 in Sequence)

"PrEEM TReQing pe chalna hai, so Come In Time."

52 Pr: President 53 E: Executive 54 E: Election 55 M: Manner 56 T: Term 57 Re: Re-election 58 Q: Qualification 59 C: Conditions 60 O: Oath 61 In: Impeachment 62 Time: Time to fill Vacancy
Article 56

Term of Office of President

  • Term of Office of President is 5 Years.
  • He can resign by addressing the resignation letter to Vice-President.
Article 57

Eligibility for Re-election of President

  • Naturalised citizen can become President (citizen by birth NOT required, unlike USA).
  • Eligible for re-election any number of times.
Article 60

Oath or Affirmation by the President

  • Administered in the presence of Chief Justice of India (CJI).
  • If CJI is unavailable, then Senior-Most Judge of the Supreme Court will take this charge.
Article 58 Qualification for Election as President
  • Must be a citizen of India.
  • Minimum 35 years of age.
  • Qualified for election as a member of Lok Sabha.
  • Must not hold any office of profit. (Office of profit is not defined in the Constitution).
📌 What is Office of Profit?
Offices in which people are appointed and removed by the government; however, offices of President, Vice President, PM, Governor (and Union/State Ministers) are NOT considered as office of profit.
Article 59

Conditions of Office of President

  1. Shall not be a member of either House of Parliament or State Legislature. If he is a member, he/she should vacate the seat before the appointment.
  2. Entitled to emoluments and allowances as determined by Parliament (shall not be diminished during his term).
Did You Know? (Privileges & Records — Page 3)
• President is immune from any criminal proceedings during his term of office.
• After giving 2 months notice, civil proceedings can be carried against him.
• Dr. Rajendra Prasad: Longest serving and the 1st President of India (1950–62).
• Dr. Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan: Served twice as Vice President (1952–62).

Article 61 Procedure for Impeachment of the President

Borrowed from: USA

Grounds of Removal: Violation of the Constitution. (Note: This phrase is NOT defined in Constitution).

  1. Impeachment charges can be initiated by either house of Parliament (LS or RS).
  2. Charges should be signed by 1/4 members of that House (which initiated the charges).
  3. A 14 days notice should be given to the President.
  4. After this, the Resolution should be passed by Special Majority by both the Houses (Majority of two-thirds of the Total Membership of that House).
  5. President has the right to appear & to be represented at such investigation.
• All Members of Parliament (elected + nominated) take part in the Impeachment process.
• MLAs participate in President's election, but NOT in impeachment.
• Impeachment is a Quasi-judicial process.
• No President has been impeached till date.

Article 62 Time of Holding Election to Fill Vacancy

Case A — If term ends normally:
Election should be held before the expiration of President's term.
Case B — If vacancy is created due to death, resignation, removal:
Election must be held within 6 months. Till then, who acts as Acting President?
  1. The Vice President acts as Acting President.
  2. If Vice President is not available, then Chief Justice of India (CJI) acts as Acting President.
  3. If CJI is also unavailable, then Senior-most Judge of the Supreme Court acts.
📌 Historical Example (1969): President Dr. Zakir Hussain died in office → VP V.V. Giri became Acting President (later resigned to contest election) → CJI M. Hidayatullah served as Acting President!

Vice-President (VP) of India: Articles 63 to 70

Article 63

Office of Vice-President

There shall be a Vice-President of India. [Covered under Articles 63–71].

Article 64

Ex-Officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha

  • Vice President shall be the ex-officio Chairman of the Council of States (Rajya Sabha).
  • VP holds second highest constitutional post.
  • VP can act as President only for a maximum period of 6 months.
Article 65

Vice-President to Act as President

In the absence of the President, the Vice President performs functions as an Acting President.

Article 66 Election & Qualifications of Vice-President

1) Elected by Electoral College:
  • All members of both Houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha).
  • Includes nominated members also (No MLAs participate).
2) Method of Election:
  • Proportional Representation + Single Transferable Vote (STV) & Secret ballot voting.
  • 20 proposers + 20 electors as seconders.
  • Required Security deposit: ₹15,000/-.
  • Each voter has multiple preferences of voting.
3) Eligibility Criteria:
  • Must be a citizen of India.
  • Must be of 35 years or more.
  • Qualified for election as a member of the Rajya Sabha.
4) Condition of Office:

He shall not be a member of either house of the parliament and/or State Legislature (Same as President).

Article 67 Term of Office & Removal of Vice-President

  • Can hold office up to 5 years.
  • Can resign to the President (and vice-versa: President resigns to VP).
  • Will continue in office until successor takes charge.
🔥 Removal Procedure of VP:
  • Resolution initiated ONLY in Rajya Sabha.
  • Passed by Effective Majority in RS (majority of all the then members) and Simple Majority in LS (agreed to by LS).
  • 14 days prior notice required.
Article 68
Time of Election to Fill Vacancy

• Normal expiration: completed before expiration of term.
• Resignation, removal, or death: Election held as soon as possible (no 6-month limit for VP).

Article 69
Oath or Affirmation by VP

Administered by the President or any person appointed on behalf of him.

Article 70
Other Contingencies

Parliament may make provisions for the discharge of the President's functions in any other contingency.

⭐ Special VP Record
Longest Serving VP

M. Hamid Ansari (2007–2017) (along with Dr. S. Radhakrishnan, 10 years). He is the only VP to serve under 3 Presidents.

Election Disputes (Art 71), Pardoning Power (Art 72) & Judicial Powers

Article 71 Matters Relating to Election of President & VP

  1. All doubts and disputes related to elections of President & VP are handled by the Supreme Court, whose decision is final.
  2. If election is declared void by the SC, then the acts done by VP/President before the date of declaration are considered valid (not invalidated).
  3. Parliament may regulate matters related to election of P/VP.
  4. Election cannot be challenged on the ground that the electoral college electing that person was incomplete (e.g., if a State Assembly was dissolved).

Judicial Powers of the President (Page 7)

  • Appoints Chief Justice and judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts.
  • Can seek advice from Supreme Court on any question of law or fact (Art 143). However, the advice tendered by the SC is NOT binding on him.
  • Exercises pardoning powers under Article 72.

Article 72 Pardoning Power of the President (5 Types)

Governor's Pardoning Power: Article 161
1. Pardon (क्षमा):
Completely removes punishment and conviction (absolves offender from all sentences).
2. Commutation (लघुकरण):
Changes the form of punishment (substitutes one form for a lighter form, e.g., death to life imprisonment).
3. Remission (परिहार):
Reduce the punishment period (without changing its character, e.g., 5 yrs rigorous to 2 yrs rigorous).
4. Respite (विराम):
Lessen the punishment due to special conditions (e.g., pregnancy or physical disability).
5. Reprieve (प्रविलंबन): Stay on execution for a temporary duration (especially of a death sentence, to allow convict time to seek pardon).
⚖️ President (Art 72) vs Governor (Art 161): The President can pardon death sentences and court-martial punishments while the Governor CANNOT.
🏛️ Kehar Singh vs Union of India Case (1988): Pardoning power of the President is not subject to judicial review, unless the decision is arbitrary, mala fide, irrational, or discriminatory.
🧠 Trick to Remember 5 Pardons: Pardon = Poora Maaf; Commutation = Character/Form Change; Remission = Reduce Time/Period (M=Minutes/Months kam); Respite = Special Condition (Pregnancy); Reprieve = Temporary Stay (Roko fansi)!

Some Important Exam Facts (High-Yield One-Liners)

• Dr. Rajendra Prasad was the longest serving President of India (12 Years, 1950–1962).
• Dr. Zakir Hussain was the first Muslim President. He died in Harness (died in office).
• V.V Giri was the first acting President to be later elected as full-term President.
• Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed died in Office (2nd to die in harness) and B.D Jatti was appointed as acting President.
• Neelam Sanjiva Reddy was the only President who was elected unopposed (1977–1982).
• Giani Zail Singh was the first Sikh President of India (1982–1987).
• R. Venkataraman had served during the Coalition era (worked with 4 PMs: Rajiv Gandhi, V.P. Singh, Chandra Shekhar, P.V. Narasimha Rao).
• K.R Narayanan was the first Dalit President of India (1997–2002).
• Dr. A.P.J Abdul Kalam was also known as 'Missile Man' and 'People's President' (2002–2007).
• Pratibha Patil was the first Woman President of India (2007–2012).
• Droupadi Murmu is the First Tribal and Second Woman President of India (2022–Present).
• Dr. Zakir Hussain was also the first Muslim Vice President of India (1962–1967).
