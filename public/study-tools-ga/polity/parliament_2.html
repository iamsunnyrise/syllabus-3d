Legislative Procedures: Article 107 & Article 108

Article 107 Introduction and Passing of Bills

  • 107(1): A Bill can originate in either House (LS or RS) except the Money bill.
  • 107(2): A Bill passes if both Houses agree, with or without amendments.
  • 107(3): Bill pending in Parliament does not lapses due to adjournment or prorogation of the session.
  • 107(4): Bills lapsing after dissolution:
    • A Bill pending in Lok Sabha and not passed at the time of its dissolution lapses.
    • A Bill pending in the RS, which has not been passed by the LS shall not lapse on the dissolution of the LS.
💡 Hinglish Mein Samjho (Lapsing Trick): Session ke Adjournment ya Prorogation se bill kabhi khatam (lapse) nahi hota! Bill tabhi lapse hota hai jab Lok Sabha Dissolve ho aur bill kisi bhi tarah LS mein atka ho (ya LS se pass hoke RS gaya ho). Agar bill Rajya Sabha mein pending hai aur LS ne abhi chhua tak nahi hai, toh wo SAFE hai (No Lapse)!

Classification of Bills

  1. Constitutional Amendment Bill
  2. Ordinary Bill
  3. Financial Bill (Some financial bills are also Money Bill)

Ordinary Bills are of Two Types

1. Public Member Bill

• 1 week prior notice should be given.
(Introduced by a Minister)

2. Private Bill
  • • 1 Month Prior notice should be given
  • • Brought by a MP who is not a minister
🧠 Trick to Remember: Mantri ji (Public Bill) busy hote hain toh sirf 1 Week notice; Non-Minister MP (Private Bill) ko poore 1 Month ka notice dena padta hai!

Article 108 Passage of Ordinary Bill & Joint Sitting of Both Houses

Source Country: Australia
Step 1: Origin

Can originate in either house (LS or RS).

Step 2: Agreement

If both the houses pass the bill, it goes to President for his Assent.

Step 3: Deadlock

When Deadlock created, President calls Joint Sitting → Bill passed by Simple Majority.

Deadlock in 3 Cases:
  1. The bill is rejected by the other house; or
  2. The houses have finally disagreed as to the amendments to be made in the bill; or
  3. More than six months elapse from the date of the reception of the bill by the other house without the bill being passed by it.

• 108(1): In case of a deadlock (disagreement between both Houses), the President may summon a Joint Sitting.

108(2): Who Presides?
1st Choice: Speaker of Lok Sabha presides over the Joint Sitting.
2nd Choice (In his absence): Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha presides.
3rd Choice (In absence of both): Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha.
⚠️ Crucial Exam Trap: Chairman of Rajya Sabha (Vice-President) CANNOT preside over the Joint sitting as he is not the member of either house.
Majority, Rules & Exceptions
  • Majority Needed: Simple majority (majority of members present and voting).
  • Rules Followed: Joint Sitting follows the Rules of Lok Sabha.
  • Exceptions (NO Joint Sitting allowed): Money Bill and Constitutional Amendment Bill.
Joint Sittings Have Happened 3 Times:
  1. Dowry Prohibition Bill, 1960
  2. Banking Services Commission Bill, 1977
  3. Prevention of Terrorism Bill (POTA), 2002
🧠 Trick to Remember 3 Joint Sittings (DBP - 60, 77, 02): Dowry (1960), Banking (1977), POTA/Terrorism (2002). Yaad rakho: Summon karta hai President, Rules lagte hain Lok Sabha ke, aur Preside karta hai Speaker!

Money Bills (Art 109–110) & Assent / Veto Powers (Art 111)

Article 109 Special Procedure for Money Bills

  • 109(1): A Money Bill can only be introduced in Lok Sabha, on the recommendation of the President.
  • 109(2): After a bill is passed to RS, it can: approve, suggest amendments or take no action on the bill, but it must give its response within 14 days.
  • 109(3): Lok Sabha may accept or reject Rajya Sabha's suggestions. If RS does not pass the bill within 14 days, it shall be deemed passed by both houses.

Some Facts About Money Bill

  • The Speaker of Lok Sabha certifies whether a Bill is a Money Bill.
  • A Bill is NOT considered as a money bill if it provides: For the imposition of fine OR imposition or abolition of any tax by local Authority.
  • The Speaker's decision is final, and normally cannot be challenged in any court. However, Judicial review is allowed.

Article 110 Definition of Money Bill (7 Provisions)

A Bill is a Money Bill if it contains ONLY provisions related to:
1. Tax Changes: Imposition, abolition, remission, alteration, regulation of any tax.
2. Borrowings: Regulation of borrowings (by Govt of India).
3. Custody & Audit: Custody and audit of Consolidated Fund or Contingency Fund.
4. Appropriation: Appropriation of money out of Consolidated Fund.
5. Charged Expenditure: Declaring any expenditure as charged on Consolidated Fund.
6. Receipt/Issue of Money: Receipt of money into Consolidated Fund of India or Public Account of India, or custody/issue of such money.
7. Incidental Matters: Any matter incidental to any of the above matters.
💡 Hinglish Mein Samjho (Art 109 vs 110): Art 109 mein Money Bill ka Procedure hai (Sirf LS mein aayega, RS ke paas sirf 14 din), aur Art 110 mein Money Bill ki Definition hai! Dhyan rahe: Traffic chalan/Fine ya Municipal/Local body tax Money Bill NAHI hota!

Article 111 Assent to Bills & Veto Powers of the President

After a bill is passed by both the Houses, the bill is then presented to the President. President may: (a) Give assent (Bill becomes a law) or (b) Use its Veto power.

Nature of Veto Power: Negative in Nature
1. Suspensive Veto
  • President returns the Bill for reconsideration. If Parliament passes it again, assent is mandatory.
  • Exception: Not available in case of Money bill because it is already brought into the Parliament after the prior recommendation of the President.
2. Pocket Veto
  • No action taken by President. (No time limit to act.)
  • First used by Gyani Zail Singh for Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill 1986.
3. Absolute Veto
  • President rejects the Bill completely.
Do You Know? (Constitutional Bill Rule) No Veto can be used in case of a Constitutional Amendment Bill: Made mandatory by 24th Constitutional Amendment (Article 368).
Do You Know? (India vs USA Pocket Veto) Pocket of Indian President is deeper than the USA's president because no time limit is mentioned in constitution for Indian president to act on the bill (USA President has 10 days).

Annual Financial Statement (Budget), Cut Motions & Grants (Art 112–116)

Article 112

Annual Financial Statement (Budget)

  • President shall cause to be laid before both the Houses, a statement of estimated receipts and expenditures.
  • The term 'Budget' is NOT mentioned in the Constitution.
Article 113

Procedure in Parliament with Respect to Estimates

No expenditure from Consolidated Fund of India be done except with the authorization by Parliament.

1. Charged on Consolidated Fund of India: Not votable (due to salaries of President, SC judges, etc.).
2. Made from Consolidated Fund of India: Subject to vote in Lok Sabha through Demands for Grants.

• Only Lok Sabha has the right to vote on Demands for Grants. RS can only discuss it.
• Demands for Grants are presented on behalf of the President.

Article 114

Appropriation Bill

  • The Appropriation Bill is introduced to authorize the withdrawal of money from the Consolidated Fund of India.
  • No amendments or voting on the Appropriation Bill amounts; it is passed as a formality after Demands for Grants are voted.
  • Cut motions are moved during the voting on Demands for Grants.

3 Types of Cut Motions (Moved during Voting on Demands for Grants)

1. Policy Cut
Reduce to Rs. 1

Moved to criticize government policy (disapproval of policy underlying the demand).

2. Economy Cut
Reduce by Specified Amount

Moved to suggest savings in the proposed expenditure.

3. Token Cut
Reduce by Rs. 100

Moved to raise a specific grievance within the sphere of responsibility of Govt.

🧠 Trick to Remember (PET — 1, Spec, 100): Policy pasand nahi aayi toh keh do "Sirf Rs. 1 denge!"; Economy (Bachat) karni hai toh Specified Amount kam karo; Token shikayat (grievance) darj karani hai toh Rs. 100 ka shagun kaat lo!

Article 115 & 116 All Types of Parliamentary Grants

1. Supplementary Grant (Art 115): Granted when the amount authorised by Parliament is found insufficient for the current financial year.
2. Additional Grant (Art 115): Granted for additional expenditure upon some new service not contemplated in the budget of that year.
3. Excess Grant (Art 115): Granted when money has been spent on any service in excess of the amount granted to that service in the budget.
4. Votes of Credit (Art 116):
  • Financial grant given to the Executive from India's resources to meet urgent financial needs.
  • Details cannot be shared ordinarily in a budget.
  • It acts like a blank cheque given to the Executive by the Lok Sabha (LS).
5. Exceptional Grants (Art 116): Special provisions made by Parliament, used to meet specific expenses not covered under the regular budgetary allocation (Also Art 116 includes Vote on Account).

6 Stages in Enactment of Budget

  1. 1 Presentation (of Budget)
  2. 2 Discussion (General Discussion)
  3. 3 Scrutiny by departmental committees
  4. 4 Voting on demands for grants
  5. 5 Passing of Appropriation bill
  6. 6 Passing of Finance bill
💡 Exam Sequence Tip: Hamesha yaad rakho pehle Appropriation Bill (Stage 5) pass hota hai (kharcha nikalne ke liye), aur sabse aakhir mein Finance Bill (Stage 6) pass hota hai (tax collection ke liye)!

Different Types of Funds (Art 266–267) & Procedure Articles (Art 117–122)

3 Different Types of Funds under Indian Constitution

Fund Name Article Consists Of / Nature Control / Managed By
1. Consolidated Fund of India Article 266 (1) Consists of: Tax & Revenue Parliamentary Authorization required (Art 113/114)
2. Public Account of India Article 266 (2) Consists of: Remittance (public fund deposits) and Judicial deposits Managed by: Executive (not under Parliament's direct control)
3. Contingency Fund of India Article 267 Unforeseen / Emergency expenditure In hands of President, but run by President and Finance Secretary
Article 117
Special Provisions for Financial Bills
  • All Money Bills are Financial Bills, but vice versa is not true.
  • Procedure of financial bill is same as ordinary bill, except that financial bill (Category A) can be introduced only in Lok Sabha and with prior recommendation of President.
Article 118
Rules of Procedure

Each House of Parliament may make rules for regulating its procedure and conduct of business.

Article 119
Regulation of Procedure for Financial Business

Parliament can regulate laws related to financial procedures.

Article 120
Language to be Used in Parliament
  • Business of Parliament shall be transacted in Hindi or in English.
  • Unless Parliament by law provides, Use of 'English' to be removed after 15 years.
  • Presiding officer may allow any member to express themselves in their mother tongue.
Article 121
Restriction on Discussion in Parliament

No discussions shall take place in Parliament with respect to the conduct of any judge of the Supreme Court or of a High Court (except upon a motion for removal).

Article 122
Court not to Inquire into Proceedings of Parliament
  • No court shall inquire into Parliament proceedings.
  • Conduct of MPs or officers not subject to court jurisdiction.

Ordinance Making Power (Art 123) & Special Powers of Rajya Sabha

Article 123 Power of President to Promulgate Ordinances during Recess

Governor (State): Article 213
When Issued: President can issue ordinances when both Houses are OR even one House is not in session.
Force of Law: Ordinance has the same force as an Act of Parliament.
Reassembly Rule (a): Must be laid before both Houses; shall cease to operate after 6 weeks from reassembly unless approved.
Withdrawal & Competence (b): Can be withdrawn anytime by the President. Should not contain provisions beyond Parliament's legislative competence (i.e. on the subjects of Union List).
⏳ Max Life of an Ordinance (Page 6):
  • If Approved: Continues as law.
  • If Disapproved: Ordinance lapses.
  • In case Parliament takes no action: Max life is 6 months and 6 weeks — that is, Max gap between sessions is 6 months, and when no action is taken 6 weeks post reassembly, the Ordinance lapses.

Special Powers of Rajya Sabha

1. Article 312

Can authorise Parliament to create a new All India Service (IAS, IPS, IFoS).

2. Article 249

Can authorise the Parliament to make a law on a Subject of State List.

🧠 Trick to Remember: President ka Ordinance = 1-2-3 (Art 123); Governor ka Ordinance = 1 aur 2 ko ulta kar do = 2-1-3 (Art 213)!

Parliamentary Committees: Financial & Departmental Standing Committees

Constitutional Status & Appointment

  • Constitution mentions these committees at different places without any specific provision regarding their tenure, functions, etc.
  • All these matters are dealt by rules of two houses.
  • A Parliamentary committee is appointed by Speaker/Chairman and works on his directions.

Classification of Parliamentary Committees

Permanent Committee
  1. Standing Committee
  2. Financial Committees (PAC, COPU, Estimates)
Temporary Committee
  1. Ad Hoc Committee

Special Note: Speaker vs Deputy Speaker
  • Speaker of Lok Sabha is the ex-officio Chairman of: Business Advisory Committee, Rules Committee, General Purpose Committee.
  • Deputy Speaker has a special distinction where he automatically becomes the chairman of a committee of which he is a member of (e.g., Committee on Private Members Bill).
