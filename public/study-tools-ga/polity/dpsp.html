Salient Features of DPSP & Famous Scholar Comments

Topics at a Glance
Salient Features of DPSP Part IV: Articles 36 to 51
  • Non-Justiciable in Nature: DPSPs are non-justiciable, which means that they are NOT legally enforceable by the courts for their violation (Aap DPSP lagu karwane ke liye court mein writ nahi daal sakte).
  • Borrowed From: The concept is borrowed from the Irish Constitution (Ireland) (which had copied it from the Spanish Constitution).
  • Core Objective: Their objective is to help establish Social and Economic Democracy in the country (jabki Fundamental Rights Political Democracy sthapit karte hain).
  • Vision of Welfare State: DPSPs reflect the vision of a Welfare State (कल्याणकारी राज्य) (and not a mere Police State) as outlined in the Constitution.
  • Benchmark for Courts: They serve as a measure/yardstick to assess and examine the constitutional validity of laws based on their alignment with constitutional ideals.
  • Sanction Behind DPSP: DPSPs have Moral and Political sanctions (kyunki sarkar ko agle chunav mein janta ko jawab dena padta hai), not legal sanctions.
⭐ Most Asked SSC Question

Dr. B. R. Ambedkar on DPSP

Dr. B. R. Ambedkar described the Directive Principles as "Novel Features" (अनोखी विशेषता) of the Indian Constitution.

Resemblance with GoI Act, 1935:

According to Dr. Ambedkar, the DPSPs resemble the "Instrument of Instructions" (अनुदेश प्रपत्र) as contained in the Government of India Act, 1935!

💡 Hinglish: 1935 ke Act mein British सरकार Governor-General aur Governors ko jo Instrument of Instructions deti thi, DPSP bilkul waisa hi hai—bas farq yeh hai ki ab yeh instructions Legislature aur Executive ko diye gaye hain!

Pages 2 & 3 • Article-by-Article Breakdown (Part 1)

DPSP Articles 36 to 43B (Welfare, Panchayats, Workers & Co-operatives)

Topics at a Glance
Article 36 Same as Art 12

Definition of "State"

• Defines the term "State" as used in Part IV.
• The meaning of "State" here is the exact same as in Article 12 under Fundamental Rights (Part III).

Article 37 Non-Justiciable

Application of the Principles

• Declares that DPSPs are non-justiciable in nature (not enforceable by any court).
• Yet, it is the duty of the State to apply these principles when making laws and policies.

Article 38 44th CA, 1978

Social Order for Promotion of Welfare

  • (a) Art 38(1): Promote people's welfare by ensuring Justice — Social, Economic, and Political.
  • (b) Art 38(2): Minimize/reduce inequalities in income, status, facilities, and opportunities (Added by 44th Amendment, 1978).
Article 39 (6 Clauses: a to f) Certain Principles of Policy to be Followed by the State
39(a): Right to adequate means of Livelihood for all citizens (men and women equally).
39(b): Fair / equitable distribution of material resources of the community for the common good.
39(c): Avoiding concentration of wealth and means of production to the common detriment.
39(d) [Exam Favourite]: Equal pay for equal work for both men and women (समान कार्य के लिए समान वेतन).
39(e): Protection of workers' health and strength and protecting children from forcible abuse/exploitation.
39(f) [42nd CA, 1976]: Opportunities for proper / healthy development of Children in conditions of freedom & dignity.
Article 39A 42nd CA, 1976

Equal Justice and Free Legal Aid

Promotes equal justice and provides free legal aid (निःशुल्क कानूनी सहायता — e.g., NALSA / Lok Adalats) to the poor and weaker sections of society.

⭐ Added by 42nd Amendment Act, 1976!
Article 40 Gandhian Principle

Organisation of Village Panchayats

The State shall take steps to organise Village Panchayats and endow them with powers to function as units of self-government (Gram Swaraj).

Article 41 Welfare Assistance

Right to Work, Education & Public Assistance

Securing the right to work, education, and public assistance in cases of unemployment, old age, sickness, and disablement (e.g., MGNREGA, Old Age Pension).

Article 42 Maternity Relief

Just & Humane Working Conditions & Maternity Relief

Provision for securing just and humane conditions of work and for Maternity Relief (प्रसूति सहायता) for women workers.

Article 43 Living Wages

Living Wages & Decent Working Conditions

Securing a living wage (निर्वाह मजदूरी), decent standard of life for all workers, and promoting cottage industries in rural areas.

Article 43A 42nd CA, 1976

Workers' Participation in Management of Industries

The State shall take steps by suitable legislation to secure the participation of workers in the management of industries.

Article 43B 97th CA, 2011

Promotion of Cooperative Societies

The State shall promote voluntary formation, autonomous functioning, democratic control, and professional management of Cooperative Societies.

Pages 3 & 4 • Article-by-Article Breakdown (Part 2)

DPSP Articles 44 to 51 (UCC, Early Childhood, Environment, Judiciary & Peace)

Topics at a Glance
Article 44 4 = 4 (Equal)

Uniform Civil Code (UCC / समान नागरिक संहिता)

"The State shall endeavour to secure for the citizens a Uniform Civil Code throughout the territory of India."

🧠 Trick: 4 aur 4 barabar hote hain = Sabke liye ek barabar civil law (Art 44)!

Article 45 86th CA, 2002

Early Childhood Care & Education (Below 6 Yrs)

86th Amendment Act, 2002 changed the subject matter of this article and shifted focus to early childhood care and education for all children until they complete the age of six years (below 6 years).

💡 0–6 yrs = Art 45 (DPSP) | 6–14 yrs = Art 21A (FR) & Art 51A(k) (FD)!

Article 46 Gandhian

Educational & Economic Interests of SCs, STs & Weaker Sections

Promotion of educational and economic interests of Scheduled Castes (SCs), Scheduled Tribes (STs), and other weaker sections, and protecting them from social injustice.

Article 47 Liquor Ban

Nutrition, Standard of Living, Public Health & Prohibition of Intoxicants

Duty of the State to raise the level of nutrition and standard of living, improve public health, and prohibit the consumption of intoxicating drinks and drugs injurious to health.

Article 48 Cow Protection

Organisation of Agriculture & Animal Husbandry

Organise agriculture and animal husbandry on modern/scientific lines, and prohibit the slaughter of cows, calves, and other milch and draught cattle.

Article 48-A 42nd CA, 1976

Protection of Environment, Forests & Wildlife

"The State shall endeavour to protect and improve the environment and to safeguard the forests and wildlife of the country." (Added by 42nd Amendment, 1976).

Article 49 Monuments

Protection of Monuments & National Heritage

Obligation of the State to protect every monument or place or object of artistic or historic interest (declared to be of national importance) from spoliation or destruction.

Article 50 50–50 Split!

Separation of Judiciary from Executive

The State shall take steps to separate the Judiciary from the Executive in the public services of the State.

🧠 Trick: Article 50 = 50-50 batwara between Judiciary and Executive!

Article 51 Foreign Policy

Promotion of International Peace and Security

Promote international peace and security, maintain just and honourable relations with nations/neighbours, foster respect for international law, and encourage arbitration.

3. Liberal-Intellectual Principles

• Prioritize individual freedoms and rights, along with promoting a strong, rational, modern state (UCC, childhood care, environment, judiciary separation, world peace).

Page 5 • Judicial Evolution & Comparison

Cases Related to DPSPs vs Fundamental Rights & Comparison Table

Topics at a Glance
Step 1 • 1951

1. Champakam Dorairajan Case (1951)

  • Supreme Court ruled that in case of conflict, Fundamental Rights (FRs) would prevail over DPSPs.
  • However, FRs are amendable by Parliament through Constitutional Amendments.
Step 2 • 1967

2. Golaknath Case (1967)

  • Supreme Court took a U-turn and ruled that Parliament CANNOT revoke, abridge, or amend Fundamental Rights even to implement DPSPs!
Step 3 • 1971 Reactions

3. Aftermath of Golaknath (24th & 25th CA)

  • 24th Amendment (1971): Empowered Parliament to amend any part of the Constitution including FRs.
  • 25th Amendment (1971) — Inserted Article 31C:
    (i) Laws implementing Socialist DPSPs under Articles 39(b) and 39(c) cannot be declared void on the ground of violating Article 14 or Article 19.
    (ii) No such law can be questioned in any court.
Step 4 • 1973 (13-Judges Bench) Basic Structure Doctrine

4. Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973)

  • A historic 13-Judges Bench ruled that Parliament CAN amend Fundamental Rights, BUT cannot disturb the "Basic Structure" of the Constitution.
  • Declared the 2nd provision (clause ii) of the 25th Amendment (Art 31C) — which restricted Judicial Review of laws — as unconstitutional and void (while upholding clause (i) giving primacy to Art 39(b) & 39(c) over Art 14 & 19!).
Step 5 • 1980 Final Balance

5. Minerva Mills Case (1980)

Supreme Court ruled that the "Indian Constitution is founded on the bedrock of the balance between Fundamental Rights and DPSPs."

👉 FRs aur DPSPs ek rath ke do pahiye hain — dono ke beech santulan (harmony) Basic Structure ka hissa hai!

Difference Between Fundamental Rights (Part III) & DPSPs (Part IV)

⚖️ Fundamental Rights (Part III: Art 12–35) 🌾 Directive Principles / DPSP (Part IV: Art 36–51)
1. Negative obligations (Prohibits the State from doing certain things). 1. Positive obligations (Directs the State to do certain things for citizens).
2. Justiciable rights (Legally enforceable by Supreme Court & High Courts). 2. Non-justiciable (Not legally enforceable by the courts).
3. Focused on establishing Political Democracy. 3. Promotes Social and Economic Democracy / Welfare.
4. Protected by Legal Sanctions. 4. Backed by Moral and Political Sanctions.
5. Oriented towards Individual Welfare. 5. Focused on Societal / Community Welfare.
6. Automatically enforceable (do not require legislation) & Courts can declare a law violating FRs as invalid/void. 6. Require legislative enactment for implementation & Courts cannot invalidate a law merely for violating DPSP.

Page 5 • Part IVA of Constitution (Article 51A)

Part IVA: Fundamental Duties — Origin, Committee & Amendments

Topics at a Glance
Part IVA • Article 51A Added in 1976
  • Borrowed From: Fundamental Duties are borrowed from the USSR (Erstwhile Soviet Union / Russia).
  • Not in Original Constitution: They were NOT originally part of the Constitution in 1950!
  • Nature: Statutory / Non-justiciable and non-enforceable directly by court writs (though Parliament can enforce them through specific laws).
  • Citizens Only (Exam Trap!): Fundamental Duties are applicable ONLY to Indian Citizens, and NOT to Foreigners!
  • 42nd Amendment (1976): Introduced through the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 (during Emergency). Initially 10 Fundamental Duties [51A(a) to 51A(j)] were added.
  • 86th Amendment (2002): Currently, there are 11 Fundamental Duties. The 11th Fundamental Duty [Article 51A(k)] was added via the 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002.
⭐ Committee Recommendation

Sardar Swaran Singh Committee (1976)

Set up by the Congress Party (Indira Gandhi Govt) in 1976 to make recommendations about Fundamental Duties during the National Emergency.

Recommended 8 Duties
42nd CA (1976) 10 Duties
86th CA (2002) 11 Duties

💡 Yaad Rakhein: Sardar Swaran Singh Committee ki sifarish par Part IVA (Article 51A) joda gaya: 1976 mein 10 Duties aur 2002 mein 11th Duty!

Article 51(A): All 11 Fundamental Duties [Clauses (a) to (k)]

Topics at a Glance
51A (a) • 1st Duty a = Anthem & Flag

To abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals and institutions, the National Flag and the National Anthem.

⚠️ Exam Trap: National Song (Vande Mataram) is NOT mentioned in 51A(a)!
51A (b) • 2nd Duty b = Bhagat Singh / Bapu

To cherish and follow the noble ideals that inspired the national struggle for freedom (Azadi ki ladai ke aadarsho ko dil mein sanjona).

51A (c) • 3rd Duty c = 3 Words (S-U-I)

To uphold and protect the Sovereignty, Unity and Integrity of India (भारत की संप्रभुता, एकता और अखंडता की रक्षा करना).

51A (d) • 4th Duty d = Defend Country

To defend the country and render national service when called upon to do so (Desh ki raksha karna aur bulaye jaane par rashtriya seva karna).

51A (e) • 5th Duty e = Equality, Brotherhood & Women's Dignity

To promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood amongst all the people of India transcending religious, linguistic and regional or sectional diversities; and to renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women (महिलाओं के सम्मान के विरुद्ध प्रथाओं का त्याग).

51A (f) • 6th Duty f = Fort / Heritage

To value and preserve the rich heritage of our composite culture (हमारी सामासिक संस्कृति की गौरवशाली परंपरा का महत्व समझना और परिरक्षण करना).

51A (g) • 7th Duty g = Greenery / Environment

To protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers and wildlife, and to have compassion for living creatures (प्राणी मात्र के प्रति दया भाव).

51A (h) • 8th Duty h = Humanism & Science

To develop the scientific temper, humanism and the spirit of inquiry and reform (वैज्ञानिक दृष्टिकोण, मानववाद और ज्ञानार्जन तथा सुधार की भावना).

51A (i) • 9th Duty i = Eye (No Violence)

To safeguard public property and to abjure violence (सार्वजनिक संपत्ति को सुरक्षित रखना और हिंसा से दूर रहना).

51A (j) • 10th Duty j = Jadeja (Excellence)

To strive towards excellence in all spheres of individual and collective activity so that the nation constantly rises to higher levels of endeavour and achievement.

51A (k) • 11th Duty (86th CA, 2002) k = Kids' Education

Duty of a parent or guardian: To provide opportunities for education to his child or ward between the age of six and fourteen (6–14) years. (Added through 86th CA, 2002).

Trick to Learn All 11 Duties (a to k), Justice Verma Committee & 6 Enforcing Acts

Topics at a Glance
🧠 Page 7 Master Mnemonic

Trick to Learn All 11 Fundamental Duties by Sub-Clause Letter (a to k)

Exam mein pucha jata hai ki 51A(g) ya 51A(i) mein kya hai!
a — Anthem aur Flag ki respect (Constitution, Flag, Anthem)
b — Bhagat Singh / Bapu ke ideals ko follow karna (Freedom Struggle)
c — C means 3 = 3 Words: Sovereignty, Unity, Integrity
d — Defend the country & National Service
e — E means 5 & Equality, Brotherhood, No derogatory practices to Women
f — Fort matlab Heritage ki value & care karna (Composite Culture)
g — Greenery — Environment, Forests, Lakes, Rivers & Wildlife protection
h — Humanism & Scientific Temper, Spirit of Inquiry
i — Eye ("Eye for an eye...") matlab Violence nahi karna & Public Property
j — Jadeja ki tarah Excellent performer in all spheres bano aur achievements karo
k — Kids' School Education (6–14 years) (86th CA, 2002)
Committee on Operationalization of FDs

Justice Verma Committee (1999)

• It identified the existence of legal provisions for the implementation of Fundamental Duties and recommended teaching school kids about their duties.

6 Laws Reflecting and Enforcing Fundamental Duties (Page 6)

1967 Enforces Duty (c)

1. The Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act (UAPA), 1967

Prevents unlawful activities that threaten the sovereignty, unity, and integrity of India.

1971 Enforces Duty (a)

2. Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971

Prohibits disrespect/insult to the Constitution, National Flag, and National Anthem.

1972 Enforces Duty (g)

3. Wildlife Protection Act, 1972

Provides legal protection to wild animals, birds, and natural environment (along with Forest Conservation Act, 1980).

1951 Enforces Duty (a)

4. Representation of the People Act (RPA), 1951

Regulates elections and upholds democratic processes (disqualifies members indulging in corrupt practices or religious enmity).

Penal Law Enforces Duties (i) & (e)

5. Indian Penal Code (IPC)

Defines crimes and prescribes punishments, protecting public order, public property, and morality (renouncing derogatory practices against women).

1955 Enforces Duty (e)

6. Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955

Eliminates untouchability and promotes social harmony, brotherhood, and equality.
