Part V: Parliament (Art 79–122) — Article 79 & Article 80

Article 79 Constitution of Parliament

🇬🇧 Parliamentary System is Borrowed from UK (Westminster Model)

There shall be a Parliament of India that consists of: The President + Two Houses (LS + RS)

1st Pillar Non-Permanent House
Lok Sabha
  • It is a Non-Permanent House.
  • Also known as 1st Chamber or Lower House or House of People (Hindi name adopted in 1954).
  • First sitting: 13 May 1952
  • Constituted on: 17 April, 1952
  • Members elected directly by the people.
2nd Pillar Integral Part
President
  • President is an integral part of Parliament.
  • No Bill becomes law without his assent.
💡 Hinglish Note: Bhale hi President kisi bhi House (LS ya RS) mein baithte nahi hain, phir bhi bina unke signature (assent) ke koi Bill Act/Law nahi banta—isliye wo Parliament ka abhinn ang (integral part) hain!
3rd Pillar Permanent House
Rajya Sabha
  • It is a Permanent House.
  • Also known as 2nd Chamber or House of Elders or Upper House or Council of States (Hindi name adopted in 1954).
  • First sitting: 13 May 1952
  • Constituted on: 3 April, 1952
  • Represents States.
  • Also known as the 'House of Elders'.
🧠 Trick to Remember (Constituted Dates vs First Sitting): Dono Houses ki First Sitting same date (13 May 1952) ko hui thi! Lekin gathan (constituted) pehle Rajya Sabha (3 April 1952) ka hua aur phir Lok Sabha (17 April 1952) ka. Aur dono ke Hindi naam (Lok Sabha & Rajya Sabha) 1954 mein adopt kiye gaye!

Article 80 Composition of Council of States (Rajya Sabha)

Total Members (Maximum Strength): 250
238 Elected Members

Representatives of States & Union Territories (indirectly elected).

12 Nominated Members

Nominated by the President from the field of Literature, Science, Arts, Social Service (LISAS).

• Present strength of RS: 245 (233 elected, 12 nominated)
• 5 seats are vacant for PoK, Aksai Chin, etc.
Representation in RS

Members are indirectly elected by means of Proportional Representation and Single Transferable Vote (STV).

📌 Exam Note: Borrowed Sources
  • Election of RS members is borrowed from South Africa.
  • Nomination of RS members is taken from Ireland.

Articles 82, 83 & 84: Delimitation, Duration & Qualifications

Article 82 Readjustment after Each Census

Delimitation Commission established after every Census (in 10 years) to divide constituencies based on population.

📖 Definition: "Delimitation" means the act or process of fixing limits or boundaries of territorial constituencies.
Important Delimitation Acts (Total 4)
1st: 1952
2nd: 1962
3rd: 1972
4th: 2002
Key Constitutional Amendments (Art 82)
  • 42th CA (1976): Seats were fixed based on 1971 Census until 2000.
  • 84th CA (2001): Freeze on Delimitation Commission extended till 2026.
  • 87th CA (2003): Minor adjustments, Delimitation according to 2001 Census.
💡 Hinglish Mein Samjho: 1981 aur 1991 ke Census ke baad koi Delimitation Commission nahi bana kyunki 42nd CA (1976) ne seats 2000 tak freeze kar di thi, aur 84th CA (2001) ne ise 2026 tak aage badha diya!

Article 83 Duration of Houses

Rajya Sabha
  • Permanent body; not subject to dissolution.
  • 1/3rd members retire every 2 years.
  • Term of members of RS is 6 years. (Constitution mein fix nahi tha, RPA 1951 se 6 years rakha gaya).
📌 2003 Rule Change: Requirement for a candidate contesting election to RS from a particular state should be an elector in that particular state was dispensed with in 2003.
Lok Sabha
  • Tenure: 5 years.
  • Note: Maximum gap between sessions cannot exceed 6 months.
  • Can be extended during national emergency (one year at a time).
  • Elections must be held within 6 months after the end of emergency.

Article 84 Qualifications for Membership

  • Must be a citizen of India.
  • Minimum required age:
    30 years (Rajya Sabha) 25 years (Lok Sabha)
  • Other qualifications as prescribed by Parliament.
🧠 Age Trick: Panchayat/Municipality = 21 yrs, Lok Sabha/MLA = 25 yrs, Rajya Sabha/MLC = 30 yrs, President/VP/Governor = 35 yrs!

Article 85: Sessions, Motions, Questions & Session Routine

Article 85 Sessions of Parliament

Sessions of Parliament, Prorogation, and Dissolution on the advice of Council of Ministers.

Parliamentary Sessions (Summoned by the President):
  • 🌱 Budget Session: February to April (Longest)
  • 🌧️ Monsoon Session: July–August
  • ❄️ Winter Session: December (Shortest)
⏰ Routine of a Session
• 11 AM: Start
• 11 AM – 12 PM: Question Hour
• 12 – 1 PM: Zero Hour (Agenda of the day is decided)
Key Parliamentary Termination Terms
1. Prorogation

Ends a session (by President).

2. Dissolution

Formal termination of the current Lok Sabha session/house (by President).

3. Adjournment

Temporary break within a session where a sitting is terminated (by Presiding Officer).

4. Adjournment Sine Die

By Presiding Officer who ends the session meeting without fixing a date for next session, happens just before prorogation.

🔥 Adjournment Motion: The adjournment motion is introduced in the Parliament to draw the attention of the house to a definite matter of urgent public importance and needs the support of 50 members to be admitted.
Some other Parliamentary Motion / Procedures
  • Closure Motion: A motion moved by a member to cut short the debate on a matter before the house.
  • Call Attention Motion: It is introduced in Parliament by a member to call the attention of a minister on a matter of public importance.
  • Point of Order: It is raised when the proceedings of the house do not follow the normal rules of procedure. No debate is allowed on Point of Order.
Types of Questions in Question Hour
  • Starred (*): Oral question-answers, can have supplementary questions.
  • Unstarred: Required written answers, No Supplementary questions could follow.
  • Short Notice: 10 days prior notice should be given.
  • Note on Private Members: Question could also be asked to private members. (A private member is the one who is not a minister.)
High-Yield Comparison

Censure Motion vs No-Confidence Motion

Censure Motion No-Confidence Motion
• Reason must be stated • No reason required
• Can be moved against an individual, group, or entire council • Can be moved against entire council only.
• To target for some specific policies/actions • To test majority support (confidence) of LS in CoM.
• If passed, no resignation required • If passed, resignation is mandatory
💡 Hinglish Mein Samjho: Censure Motion ka matlab hai sarkar ya kisi ek mantri ko kisi galti par daant lagana (reason batana zaroori hai, par pass hone par sarkar girti nahi hai). Jabki No-Confidence Motion poori Council of Ministers ke khilaf aata hai aur pass hote hi poori sarkar ko resign karna padta hai!

Articles 86 to 99: President's Address & Officers of Parliament

Article 86

Right of President to Address and Send Messages

  • President can address either House or both Houses jointly.
  • President can send messages regarding pending Bills or other matters.
Article 87

Special Address by the President

  • President addresses both Houses: At the first session after each general elections and the first session of each fiscal year.
  • Motion of Thanks follows the address.
Article 88

Rights of Ministers and Attorney General

  • Ministers: Right to speak in either House but can vote only in that house of which they are a member.
  • Attorney General: Not a member of either house but has the right to speak in LS/RS, without having Right to Vote.
Rajya Sabha Officers (Art 89–92) Chairman & Deputy Chairman
Article 89: Chairman and Deputy Chairman of RS
  • Chairman: Vice President of India (Ex-officio). He is not a member of RS.
  • Deputy Chairman: Elected by Rajya Sabha. Fulfills duties as the Acting Chairman in absence of the Chairman. He is a member of RS.
  • While the Chairman is presiding the house, Deputy Chairman is like any other member of the house.
Article 90: Vacation, Resignation, Removal of Dy Chairman
  • Resignation: By writing to the Chairman.
  • Removal: By Effective majority of the total membership of the Rajya Sabha.
  • 📐 Formula: Effective Majority = Majority of the (total membership of house − Vacancies)
Article 91: Power/Duty of Deputy Chairman
  • Presides in absence of Chairman.
  • When Seats of Chairman and Deputy Chairman are vacant: Then President can choose any member from the house to discharge duties of RS.
  • If the Chairman and DC, both are absent, then: One member of the Panel of Vice-Chairpersons, appointed by the Chairman, presides. (This is not explicitly mentioned in Constitution).
  • 🏅 P.T. Usha: First woman to be appointed in the RS panel of Vice-Chairpersons.
Article 92: Not to Preside During Their Removal
  • They can participate in proceedings but cannot preside during their own removal resolution.
  • Can vote in first instance (Deputy Chairman) but not in the case of tie (casting vote exercised by Presiding Officer).
  • Chairman does not have the casting vote rights in this case, as he is not a member of RS (in fact, he cannot vote at all during his removal resolution).
Lok Sabha Officers (Art 93–96) Speaker & Deputy Speaker
Article 93: Speaker and Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha
  • Elected by Lok Sabha members by Simple Majority.
  • First Speaker: Ganesh Vasudev Mavalankar.
  • First Deputy Speaker: M. A. Ayyangar.
  • Note: No Deputy Speaker appointed since 2019.
  • 📅 Date of election of Speaker is fixed by the President, and that of Deputy Speaker is fixed by Speaker.
Article 94: Vacation, Resignation, Removal of Speaker & Dy Speaker
  • Resignation: Speaker resigns to Deputy Speaker and vice-versa.
  • Removal: By Effective majority.
  • Speaker continues till a new Speaker is elected; he does not immediately resign after the dissolution of LS and continues till the newly elected LS meets.
Article 95: Powers to Perform Duties
  • Deputy Speaker presides when Speaker is absent.
  • If the Seat is vacant: Then President can choose any member to discharge the duties.
  • If the Speaker and Deputy Speaker, both are absent: Then one member of the Panel of Chairpersons, appointed by the Speaker presides.
Article 96: Speaker & Dy Speaker Not to Preside During Removal
  • They can participate in proceedings but cannot preside while a motion for their removal is under discussion.
  • The Speaker's right to vote in the first instance is retained, but has no right to casting vote in the case of tie.
  • Note: Deputy speaker is not subordinate to the Speaker (he is directly responsible to the Lok Sabha).
Article 97
Salaries and Allowances

Salaries of Chairman and Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha and Speaker and Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha are decided by Parliament.

Article 98
Secretariat of Parliament

• Each House has its separate secretarial staff.
• Secretariat appointed by President on advice of the Speaker of the LS and Chairman of RS.

Article 99
Oath or Affirmations by Members

Every member of either House must take oath before taking his seat as administered by the President or a person appointed by him.

Protem Speaker
Temporary Presiding Officer

• Senior-most member appointed temporarily by President to administer oath to members.
• Bhartruhari Mehtab has served as Protem Speaker from 24th June 2024 to 26th June 2024 after 2024 General Elections.

Articles 100 to 106 (Quorum, Defection, Privileges) & Article 73

Article 100 Voting in Houses and Quorum

  • 100(1): The Chairman or Speaker shall not vote in the 1st instance but shall exercise a casting vote in case of a tie.
  • 100(3) Quorum: Minimum 1/10th of total strength of House is required to carry on the proceedings (i.e., 55 in LS, 25 in RS).
  • 100(4): If quorum is not met, house is adjourned by the presiding officer.

Article 101 Vacation of Seats

  • 101(1): No person shall be a member of both Houses of Parliament.
  • 101(2): No person can be a member of State Legislature and a member of Parliament.
  • 101(3): Members can be disqualified as mentioned in Article 102.
  • (101 3A): A member can resign his seat to the Chairman or Speaker.
  • 101(4): If a member of either of the Houses of Parliament is absent without permission for a period of 60 days, the house may declare the seat vacant.

Article 102 Disqualifications for Membership

Constitutional + RPA 1951 + 10th Schedule
102(1): A person shall be disqualified for membership if:
  • (a) He holds any office of profit under the Union or State Government.
  • (b) He is of unsound mind, as declared by a competent court.
  • (c) He is an undischarged insolvent.
  • (d) He is not a citizen of India.
⚖️ RPA: Representation of People Act, 1951: Jail for more than two years leads to disqualification.
102(2): Disqualification under 10th Schedule — Defection on the grounds of:
  • Independent members join any political party.
  • Nominated members join a political party after 6 months from the date of his nomination.
  • If he votes contrary to the direction of his party (whip).
  • If the member voluntarily gives up the membership of the party on whose ticket he has been elected.
✅ Exception: Merger when 2/3rd members leave and join other political party (91st CA, 2003).
📌 Authority on Defection: Decision of the Speaker / Chairman is final with regard to disqualification under defection.
⚖️ Kihoto Hollohan vs Zachilhu Case (1992): Judiciary can review the Final decision of the Speaker in case of disqualification under Defection.

Article 103 Decision on Questions of Disqualification

  • 103(1): Decision of the President shall be deemed final if question arises over disqualification of any member of either house (under Art 102(1)).
  • 103(2): President must obtain the opinion of the Election Commission before giving his final decision.

Article 104 Penalty for Sitting and Voting

Penalty for Sitting and Voting When Disqualified or before making an oath or affirmation under Article 99:

• If any person sits or votes knowing they are disqualified, a penalty of Rs. 500 per day is charged for the period he/she sat or voted.

Article 106 Salaries and Allowances of Members

Parliament may by law fix salaries and allowances of members of both the Houses.

Article 105 Powers, Privileges, and Immunities of MPs

  • 105(1): Freedom of speech in Parliament.
  • 105(2): No proceedings in Parliament can be questioned in any court of law. No MP liable for anything said or any vote given in Parliament.
  • 105(3): MPs enjoy protection from civil arrest during a session and 40 days before and after the session (by parliamentary convention).
⚠️ Note 1: This protection does not apply to criminal arrest or preventive detention.
⚠️ Note 2: Protection of the President from criminal proceedings is under Article 361, not Article 105.
From the Previous Lecture (PM & CoM) Article 73

Article 73: Extent of Executive Power of the Union

Subject to the provisions of this Constitution, the executive power of the Union shall extend:

  • (a) to the matters with respect to which Parliament has power to make laws; and
  • (b) to the exercise of such rights, authority and jurisdiction as are exercisable by the Government of India by virtue of any treaty or agreement.
