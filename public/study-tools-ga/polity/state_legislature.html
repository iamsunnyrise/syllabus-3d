Articles 153 to 162

The State Executive & Governor of States

Nominal Head of State
State Executive consists of 4 pillars: 1. Governor + 2. Chief Minister + 3. Council of Ministers + 4. Advocate General
⚠️ Note: No Vice-Governor in India!
Article 153 Governor of States

There shall be a Governor for each State.

⭐ 7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956: Allowed the appointment of the same person as Governor of two or more states!
Article 154 Executive Power

The executive power of the State is vested in the Governor. It is exercised directly or through officers subordinate to him in accordance with the Constitution.

Article 155 Appointment

Governor is appointed by the President of India by warrant under his hand and seal (Canadian model of appointed Governor, NOT US elected model).

Article 156 Term of Office
  • Holds office during the pleasure of the President (no security of tenure).
  • Resignation addressed to the President.
  • Normal term: 5 years from assuming office.
Article 157 Qualifications

Constitution mein sirf 2 qualifications hain:

  1. Must be a Citizen of India.
  2. Must have completed 35 years of age.

Conventions: Bahar ka resident ho (outsider) + CM consulted.

Article 158 Conditions of Office
  • Cannot be an MP or MLA/MLC.
  • Cannot hold office of profit.
  • Rent-free official residence (Raj Bhavan).
  • Emoluments fixed by Parliament; cannot be reduced during tenure.
  • If serving 2+ states, salary shared as ordered by President.
Article 159: Oath CJI of High Court

Oath is administered by the Chief Justice of the High Court of that state, or in his absence, the senior-most judge of the High Court.

⚠️ Trap: Governor ko oath President nahi dilwate! High Court ke CJ dilwate hain!
Article 160: Contingencies President Appoints

President can make provisions as he thinks fit for the discharge of Governor's functions in unforeseen contingencies.

Article 161: Pardoning Power Pardons & Commutes

Governor can grant pardons, reprieves, respites, remissions, or commute sentences for offenses against state laws.

🚨 2 Differences vs President (Art 72):
1. Governor CANNOT pardon death sentence (Only President can).
2. Governor has NO power over Court Martial (Military court) sentences.
03
Articles 163 to 167

Chief Minister, Council of Ministers & Advocate General

Real Executive Authority
Article 163: Council of Ministers to Aid & Advise Head: Chief Minister

There shall be a Council of Ministers with the Chief Minister at the head to aid and advise the Governor.

⭐ Huge Difference between Art 74 & Art 163:

President ke liye Cabinet ki advice binding hoti hai (42nd/44th Amendment). Par Governor ke paas Constitutional Discretion hoti hai! Jahan Governor apne discretion se act karta hai, wahan CoM ki advice binding nahi hoti, aur Governor ka decision final hota hai!

Article 164: Provisions as to Ministers Collective Responsibility
  • CM appointed by Governor; other Ministers appointed by Governor on advice of CM.
  • Collective Responsibility: Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha) of the state.
  • 6-Month Rule: Non-member can become Minister/CM, but must become MLA/MLC within 6 consecutive months, otherwise ceases to be a Minister.
  • Oaths of office and secrecy administered by Governor.
  • Salaries determined by the State Legislature.
Article 165: Advocate General for the State Highest Law Officer of State

Corresponds to Attorney General of India (Art 76).

  • Appointed by: Governor.
  • Qualification: Must be qualified to be appointed as a High Court Judge (Citizen, 10 yrs judicial office or 10 yrs advocate in HC).
  • Tenure: Not fixed by Constitution. Holds office during the pleasure of the Governor.
  • Remuneration: Determined by Governor.
Article 166: Conduct of Business In Name of Governor

All executive actions of the Government of a State shall be expressed to be taken in the name of the Governor.

Article 167: Duties of Chief Minister Channel of Communication

CM acts as the chief bridge between CoM and Governor: communicates all decisions of administration and proposals for legislation to the Governor.

Articles 168 to 170

State Legislature Structure & The 6 Bicameral States

Vidhan Sabha & Parishad
Article 168: Constitution of Legislatures in States Unicameral vs Bicameral

Har state mein ek Legislature hoga jisme Governor + Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha) honge, aur jahan do houses hain wahan Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad) bhi hoga.

Parmar Sir's Mnemonic Trick for 6 Bicameral States: "KUMBAT" (or "AT BUMK")
K Karnataka
U Uttar Pradesh
M Maharashtra
B Bihar
A Andhra Pradesh
T Telangana

*Notice: Jammu & Kashmir had LC before 2019 Reorganisation Act. Currently only these 6 states have Vidhan Parishad.

🏛️ 3 UTs with Legislative Assemblies: Delhi, Puducherry, and Jammu & Kashmir. Total UTs: 8 (Remaining 5 have no Assembly)
Article 169: Creation or Abolition of Legislative Council Very High-Yield

Agar kisi state mein Vidhan Parishad banana ya hatana ho, toh process kya hai?

Step 1 (State Assembly): State Assembly passes resolution by Special Majority (Majority of total membership + 2/3rd present & voting).
Step 2 (Parliament): Parliament passes law creating/abolishing it by Simple Majority!
⚠️ Exam Favorite Fact: Under Art 169(3), this law is NOT considered an amendment under Article 368!
Article 170: Composition of Legislative Assemblies (Vidhan Sabha) Directly Elected
  • Members directly elected from territorial constituencies.
  • Strength Limits: Minimum 60 members, Maximum 500 members.
  • Constituencies based on equal population-to-seat ratio.
Special Exception States (Less than 60):
• Sikkim: 32 members (min 30)
• Goa: 40 members
• Mizoram: 40 members
• Nagaland: 46 members
• Arunachal Pradesh: 60 (min 30 in law)
• Puducherry (UT): 30 elected + 3 nom

Articles 171 to 177

Composition of Legislative Council (Article 171) & Terms

5-Way Proportional Breakdown
Strength Rules of Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad):
  • Maximum strength: Shall not exceed 1/3rd of total strength of Legislative Assembly of that state.
  • Minimum strength: Cannot be less than 40 members.

Article 171(3): How Members of Legislative Council are Chosen:

1/3rd Elected

Local Authorities

Elected by electorates consisting of members of Municipalities, District Boards, and other local authorities.

1/12th Elected

Graduates

Elected by persons residing in the state who have been Graduates for at least 3 years.

1/12th Elected

Teachers

Elected by persons engaged for at least 3 years in teaching in educational institutions not lower than secondary school.

1/3rd Elected

By MLAs

Elected by members of Legislative Assembly (MLAs) from amongst persons who are NOT members of the Assembly.

1/6th Nominated

By Governor

Nominated by Governor from 5 fields: Literature, Science, Art, Cooperative Movement, Social Service.

⭐ Parmar Trick for Governor's 5 Fields: "LiSAS + Cooperative" (President nominates in RS for 4 fields: LISAS. Governor has 1 extra field: Cooperative Societies!)
Article 172: Duration Assembly vs Council

• Legislative Assembly: 5 years from date of first meeting unless dissolved earlier.
• Legislative Council: Permanent body, not subject to dissolution. 1/3rd members retire every 2nd year. Individual member term = 6 years.

Article 173: Qualifications Ages: 25 vs 30

• Citizen of India.
• MLA (Vidhan Sabha): Minimum 25 years of age.
• MLC (Vidhan Parishad): Minimum 30 years of age.
• Other qualifications as prescribed by Parliament (RPA 1951).

Article 174: Sessions Max Gap: 6 Months

• Governor summons houses.
• Maximum gap between two sessions: 6 months.
• Governor can prorogue houses or dissolve the Legislative Assembly.

Article 175: Address/Messages Governor's Right

Governor has the right to address either House or both houses assembled together, and to send messages regarding pending bills.

Article 176: Special Address 2 Occasions

Governor addresses Legislature at: (1) commencement of first session after general election, and (2) first session of each calendar year (Budget session).

Article 177: Ministers & AG Rights Speak vs Vote

Ministers and Advocate General can speak & take part in proceedings of either House or any committee, but CANNOT vote unless they are members of that House.

Articles 178 to 195

Presiding Officers, Voting, Quorum & Disqualifications

Legislative Machinery
Legislative Assembly Officers Speaker & Deputy Speaker
  • Article 178: Assembly chooses Speaker and Deputy Speaker from among its members.
  • Article 179: Resignation: Speaker gives resignation to Deputy Speaker, and Deputy Speaker to Speaker. Removed by resolution passed by majority of all the then members with 14 days' prior notice.
  • Article 180: Deputy Speaker acts as Speaker when office vacant or absent.
  • Article 181: Speaker/Deputy Speaker cannot preside when resolution for their own removal is under consideration (though they can speak and vote in first instance).
Legislative Council Officers Chairman & Deputy Chairman
  • Article 182: Council chooses Chairman and Deputy Chairman from among its members. *(Note: Unlike Rajya Sabha where Vice President is ex-officio Chairman, Vidhan Parishad Chairman is chosen from its OWN members!)*
  • Article 183: Vacation/Removal similar to Speaker (resign to each other; 14 days notice).
  • Article 184: Deputy Chairman acts as Chairman when vacant/absent.
  • Article 185: Chairman/Deputy Chairman cannot preside when resolution for their removal is being considered.
Article Key Provision Detailed Concept & Exam Points
Article 186 Salaries of Presiding Officers Fixed by State Legislature, charged upon Consolidated Fund of the State (non-votable).
Article 187 Secretariat of Legislature Each House has its separate secretarial staff; regulated by State Legislature.
Article 188 Oath by Members Administered by the Governor or person appointed by him before taking seat.
Article 189 Voting & Quorum ⭐ (1) Majority of members present and voting.
(2) Presiding officer votes only in case of a tie (Casting Vote).
(4) Quorum: 10 members or 1/10th (10%) of total membership, whichever is greater!
Article 190 Vacation of Seats Seat becomes vacant if member is elected to both Houses, resigns, or is absent for 60 consecutive days without permission.
Article 191 Disqualifications Office of profit, unsound mind, undischarged insolvent, non-citizen, or disqualified under 10th Schedule (Anti-defection law).
Article 192 Decision on Disqualification ⭐ Decided by the Governor whose decision is final, but Governor MUST obtain opinion of Election Commission of India (ECI) and act according to such opinion! *(Note: 10th Schedule defection is decided by Speaker/Chairman, not Governor!)*
Article 193 Penalty for Illegal Sitting Penalty of ₹500 per day if person sits or votes without taking oath or when disqualified.
Article 194 Powers & Privileges Freedom of speech in Legislature; immunity from court proceedings for anything said or vote given.

Articles 196 to 199

Legislative Procedure: Passing of Bills & The "No Joint Sitting" Rule

#1 Most Asked SSC Topic
THE ULTIMATE EXAM TRAP: NO JOINT SITTING IN STATE LEGISLATURE!

Parliament mein Article 108 ke tahat President deadlock solve karne ke liye Joint Sitting bula sakta hai. Lekin Constitution ke Part VI mein State Legislature ke liye Joint Sitting ka KOI PROVISION NAHI HAI! State mein Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha) supreme hoti hai!

Article 196 & 197: Life Cycle of an Ordinary Bill in Bicameral State

Case 1: Bill Originates in Legislative Assembly

Vidhan Sabha bill pass karke Vidhan Parishad (LC) ko bhejti hai. Ab LC ke paas kya powers hain?

1st Instance (Pehli Baar): LC bill ko reject kar de, ya amend kare jo LA ko manjoor na ho, ya 3 Months tak koi action na le ➔ LA bill ko dobara pass karti hai!
2nd Instance (Doosri Baar): Dobara pass hokar aane par LC use sirf 1 Month tak delay kar sakti hai! 1 month baad bill dono houses se pass maan liya jata hai!
🎯 Total Delay by Legislative Council = 3 Months + 1 Month = MAX 4 MONTHS! LC bill ko cancel nahi kar sakti!
Case 2: Bill Originates in Legislative Council

Agar bill pehle Vidhan Parishad (LC) mein originate hota hai aur pass hokar Vidhan Sabha (LA) mein jata hai:

If Assembly Rejects the Bill: Bill immediately LAPSES (khatam ho jata hai)!

Vidhan Parishad ke paas Legislative Assembly ko force karne ki ZERO power hai. Assembly agar reject karegi toh bill wahi dead ho jayega!

Articles 198 & 199: Money Bills in State Legislature Exactly like Lok Sabha
  • Money Bill can be introduced ONLY in the Legislative Assembly, never in Legislative Council.
  • Requires prior recommendation of the Governor.
  • Speaker of Legislative Assembly decides whether a bill is a Money Bill (Art 199).
  • Legislative Council cannot reject or amend; can only recommend.
  • LC must return the bill within 14 days!
  • If LC does not return within 14 days, bill is deemed passed in original form.

Articles 200 to 213

Governor's Assent, Reservation for President, Budget & Ordinance

Veto & Financial System
Article 200: Governor's Assent to Bills (4 Options) Art 200
1. Give Assent

Bill becomes an Act immediately.

2. Withhold Assent

Bill ends and does not become an Act.

3. Return for Reconsideration

If not Money Bill. If passed again by Assembly, Governor must give assent!

4. Reserve for President ⭐

Mandatory if bill endangers constitutional position of State High Court!

Article 201: Bills Reserved for President's Consideration Art 201

Jab Governor bill President ke liye reserve kar deta hai, toh President kya kar sakta hai?

• President assent de sakta hai ya withhold kar sakta hai.
• President Governor ko bill lautaane ka nirdesh de sakta hai (state legislature ko 6 months mein reconsider karna hoga).
• ⭐ HUGE TRAP: Agar State Legislature bill ko dobara pass kar de, tab bhi President assent dene ke liye BOUND NAHI HAI! (Governor bound hota hai, par President bound nahi hota!).
Article 202: Annual Financial Statement State Budget (112 + 90)

Governor causes to be laid before the Legislature the Annual Financial Statement of the state. Word "Budget" is not mentioned in Constitution.

Articles 203-207: Grants & Financial Bills Governor Recommendation
  • Art 203: Demands for Grants made only on recommendation of Governor.
  • Art 204: Appropriation Bill (no money withdrawn from Cons. Fund without it).
  • Art 205: Supplementary, Additional & Excess Grants.
  • Art 206: Vote on Account & Vote of Credit.
Article 213: Governor's Ordinance Making Power (123 + 90) Recess of Legislature
• Promulgated only when both houses (or single house) are not in session.
• Must be satisfied that circumstances require immediate action.
• Same force and effect as an Act of State Legislature.
• Life of Ordinance: Must be approved within 6 weeks from reassembly of Legislature!
• Maximum Life: 6 months + 6 weeks.
• Cannot make ordinance on matters requiring President's prior sanction without President's instructions.
Remaining Key Articles in Chapter III & IV of Part VI:
• Art 210: Language (Hindi, English, or Mother Tongue allowed by Speaker).
• Art 211: No discussion on conduct of HC/SC judges.
• Art 212: Courts not to inquire into proceedings of Legislature.
• Articles 214-231: High Courts in the States | Articles 233-237: Subordinate Courts
